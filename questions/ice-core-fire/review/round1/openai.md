**The plan can answer a narrower question than its title:** how a particular 300-year block ranks among disjoint blocks of the *measured concentration record* in each core. That is worth reporting. The biggest problem is the step from those ranks to a verdict about **one fire at the onset**. The rule can pass on two different peaks centuries apart, and fail on a common short pulse that the three records resolve very differently.

### 1. What could create or hide an exceptional peak?

- **must fix — Identify the event independently of the statistic.** The maximum anywhere in `[12,750, 13,050)` need not be the ammonium feature claimed in the literature. Two cores can pass on the same measure with one maximum near the young end and one near the old end. Even within GISP2, “the cited sample” is implemented as *the largest sample in the window*, not the sample at the cited depth. Report the cited depth interval, all samples intersecting it, the bins receiving their signal, and the locations and age supports of the winning bins. Decide before the run whether temporal agreement is required. If it cannot be established, call the result high **window** ranks, not corroboration of one peak.

- **must fix — Equal output bin lengths do not give equal effective resolution.** For example, a high GISP2 sample nearly 20 years long could fill one G20 bin, or contribute half its signal to each of two bins otherwise at background. The maxima differ even though both configurations satisfy the 18.6-year duration criterion. There is smoothing first by the physical measurement, then by binning. For CFA, 1 mm is the *reporting interval*, not a sequence of independent 1 mm measurements; a roughly centimetre-scale response spans different numbers of years at different depths. The quoted layer thicknesses derived from a 20-year age grid are not guaranteed minima for individual years. The long **decadal** series need the resolution audit too. Relative to much older, thinner ice the target may be **advantaged**; relative to recent thick ice it may be disadvantaged. “All windows are diluted alike” is incorrect.

- **must fix — Separate absolute dating uncertainty from registration within a core.** A common shift of the GICC05 calendar can move the ammonium feature and the NGRIP stadial boundary together without moving them apart in depth. Conversely, interpolation between 20-year knots does not locate N1 bins to the year. NEEM transfer onto a common chronology is another source of relative uncertainty. GISP2 is being centred on a *reported chemical feature*, not demonstrably on an independently picked climatic boundary; this gives it a different opportunity to score well. Specify which comparison is depth-relative, which is calendar-relative, the BP origins and age-model versions, and which alignments are actually known. Age-scale *stretch* changes sample durations, peak dilution, backgrounds and the number of comparison windows, not “only window boundaries.” Do not jitter each bin independently by the maximum counting error.

- **must fix — A whole-record reference and an onset-like reference answer different questions.** Holocene, stadial and interstadial ice differ in source strength, snowfall, transport, background variability and sampling. E10 and G60 include the recent Holocene, N10 does not; G20 for Z stops at 50 ka. Thus even the three core percentiles for one measure have different comparison populations. Keep the whole-record ranks for the stated descriptive question, but also prespecify common-span and comparable-background/resolution controls. Show the target profile across the transition. A centred median that straddles a climate step can turn an ordinary value on the high side into an “excess,” or subtract much of a sustained enhancement.

- **should fix — Test bin phase separately from window phase.** The specified grid is reproducible in principle, but it is not “every other stretch.” A control episode can be split between two blocks; the nominated episode was deliberately put near the middle of one. The GISP2 centre is at the middle of a G60 bin but at an edge of a G10 bin. A 300-year search does not restore “full height” to a peak split or smoothed at the bin level. The 120/600-year runs change block length, comparison population and, for some series, bin phase simultaneously. Vary one at a time, on grids the data actually support; give controls the same alignment opportunities as the target. Do not select the target's best phase after looking.

- **must fix — Match the number of opportunities to produce a maximum.** An H from 24 observed decadal bins competes with one from 30; an E or Z from just one finite score could compete with one from 30 under a reasonable reading of the plan. At T = 60 the 80% rule permits four of five bins, each itself potentially only half covered. Missingness may track core condition, depth or analytical problems. Report coverage by age and **by measure**, require a minimum amount of valid support for the measure itself, and repeat with near-complete blocks. Show whether the claimed feature itself is covered; 80% coverage elsewhere in its block is no substitute.

### 2. Measures and verdicts

- **must fix — H, E and Z are not three ways of establishing high ammonium loading.** H is a concentration; E still depends on the *scale* of local variability; Z can be enormous for a modest concentration in a quiet, rounded series with tiny nonzero log-MAD. Self-inclusion in a short running background can also suppress a broad peak. Inspect and report the numerator, denominator, positive count and concentration of winning Z bins; prespecify a sensitivity to measurement precision or unstable scale. Choose which quantity supports which claim. In particular, winning on Z alone should not be described as one of the largest concentration peaks. None measures burnt area or even deposition flux.

- **must fix — Exchangeability alone does not justify “about 2%.”** With independent cores and continuous uniform marginal ranks, the probability of at least two top-5% results on **one** measure is \(3(.05)^2(.95)+(.05)^3=0.00725\). Three such measures give about 2.2% under further independence, or at most about 2.2% by a union bound under the independent-core calculation. But if core ranks track each other perfectly, the one-measure probability is already 5%, even with exchangeable windows. Shared climate, transport and chronology matter at least as much as shared laboratory. The 9% figure has a different basis: a loose union bound for nine marginal 1% opportunities, subject to ties and integer cutoffs. Drop the chance language or calibrate a clearly specified *joint* rule. A single decoy run does not calibrate it.

- **must fix — The failure labels overstate the evidence.** “One core only” can mean two or even three cores reached 1% on *different* measures. “Ordinary” can include a striking result in one core just outside its 1% cutoff; it does not imply that many windows beat the target in every core. Missing or untestable cores have no specified effect on the joint verdict. Do not convert inability to pass into a negative finding. Report the core-by-measure matrix, specify when the joint question is indeterminate, and distinguish “did not meet the criterion” from evidence against a short synchronous pulse.

- **should fix — Make the cutoff and the headline honest at small \(N\).** G20 has at most about 166 complete 300-year windows before losses, so its 1% criterion requires rank 1, not rank 2. For \(N<100\), `max(1, …)` can admit more than 1%; strict-larger ranking can admit an arbitrarily large tied group. Give \(N\), counts strictly above **and tied**, and realized tail fractions. The ranks are of block maxima, not of distinct fires. A Z verdict can rely on only 50 ka in GISP2. N3 covers about **49,700 years**, not 60,000 years of observations. A selectively promoted fine-series rank 1 should name its measure, core, actual span and the other fine-series results—not be headlined as “the largest peak” regardless of measure.

- **should fix — Separate ascertainment from blinding.** Not inspecting the onset concentrations is good practice. It does not make the target a randomly nominated interval: the question and GISP2 anchor were selected because a peak had been reported. This is a test of a previously nominated feature, not a blind discovery with the quoted false-alarm odds.

2. **must fix — Quantify recovery on the real sampling geometry.** Pass identical synthetic short pulses of specified duration and integrated signal through actual GISP2 sample intervals and NGRIP depth-age grids at several ages and phases, bracketing plausible CFA smoothing. Compare recovered maxima and loss rates. For decadal-only products, acknowledge what cannot be recovered. Add a comparison degraded to a common effective resolution where feasible. 3. **must fix — Audit the winning features, not just ranks.** Prespecify a compact profile/table for the target and leading controls: raw depths or source bins, valid support, H/E/Z peak ages, background, MAD and neighbouring values.
