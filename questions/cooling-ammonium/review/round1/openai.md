### Decision rules

1. **Must fix — the binomial reference is unsupported.** A top-quarter cut labels roughly a quarter of the *reference* distribution, subject to ties. It does not give each cooling an independent 0.25 probability of passing. Events share an estimated reference distribution and can cluster by age and climate regime. Overlap of quiet windows is fine for describing a time-weighted percentile; it does not produce thousands of independent null examples. Either keep the counts descriptive, or calibrate the **count itself** with sets of matched pseudo-events, preserving temporal structure and using the same pseudo-dates for both cores. State the exchangeability assumption and where comparable dates are scarce.

2. **Must fix — “in both cores” does not require the same events to pass.** With 24 evaluable events, 16 successes per core qualify as *General* even if only **eight** coolings pass in both. Report paired outcomes: both, NGRIP only, NEEM only, neither, and unavailable. If replication of an event response matters, apply the criterion to paired events. Conversely, the two cores are not independent draws of Greenland climate; agreement is useful, but their probabilities should not be multiplied.

3. **Must fix — the negative verdicts assert too much.** Twenty-four small real rises can fail a test for unusually large peaks. “Not seen” conflates little evidence, low sensitivity, core disagreement and evidence of absence. Similarly, rank 1 in one core and just outside the top quarter in the other yields “ordinary.” Report the counts, ranks, effect sizes and discordance. Use an inconclusive category unless there is an explicit, adequately sensitive test of absence or ordinariness.

4. **Must fix — raw rank is not a calibrated anomaly test.** GS-1 was selected because of its profile, and the search window was chosen to contain it. This affects its **rank interpretation**, not just its own quiet percentile. Raw P also rewards low baselines and more variable or better-resolved ice. Rank 1 by a negligible margin passes; rank 2 in both cores by a negligible margin fails. Give the gap to comparable events and the stability of ranks under dating, resolution and baseline changes. Distinguish “largest measured ratio in this catalogue” from “most unusual relative to its background.”

5. **Should fix — the combined verdicts conflict.** *General + among the largest* means “usual” in “How the two combine,” but “in between” in “What would count.” More fundamentally, a response can be common **and** GS-1 exceptionally large. Keep prevalence and GS-1 magnitude as separate answers. Mark R, 300-year windows and excluded-event analyses as descriptive sensitivities, not alternative routes to a preferred verdict.

6. **Must fix — calcium is a positive control for a narrower claim.** Recovering a strong dust transition is useful for detecting gross age-direction, indexing or alignment errors. It does not show power for a weak, delayed, short-lived ammonium signal: calcium may remain elevated even with a badly misplaced boundary. Moreover, the event stratigraphy itself uses dust/calcium information, and the control result was known before lock. Retain it as a pipeline check, not validation of the ammonium thresholds, timing or null.
