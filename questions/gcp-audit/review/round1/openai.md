### Reproduction and event selection

1. **Must fix — resolve the window endpoints.** The quoted registry convention is `[begin, end)`; the plan uses `end − begin + 1`. This changes the observations, not just a label: an end at midnight can add a day-file requirement, a blocked second and a whole day to a reference pool. Conversely, a table entry ending at `23:59:59` may have been intended to include that second. Before scoring, check the literal times against intended durations and published degrees of freedom where available. Freeze one primary convention and any documented exceptions; show the other convention as a sensitivity, not whichever fits each event better.

2. **Must fix — establish what calculation each selected row purportedly reproduces.** A generic description of squaring a network Stouffer score does not explain the registry field **“Chisquare 600 df weight”**, prove that the two recipe strings are interchangeable, or specify the historical conversion from statistic to signed event Z. Check the available recipe descriptions and intermediate table fields (duration, df, statistic or probability where given). Separate discrepancies *before* the tail conversion from discrepancies *in* the conversion. If masking, scaling or weighting cannot be reconstructed, a poor match is a failure of **this reconstruction**, not evidence that the table did not follow from the raw files.

3. **Must fix — the reproduction labels do not measure per-event agreement.** For example, `ours = published_mean + 2 × (published − published_mean)` passes “closely” perfectly: correlation 1, identical combined score, wrong deviations for nearly every event. Conversely, for 396 events the combined-score tolerance of 1 allows a mean signed discrepancy of only **0.050 Z per event**; a small consistent implementation difference can fail that gate. Make an absolute-agreement measure already in the plan, such as a required fraction within a justified tolerance, part of the decision. Report signed bias, slope, large individual discrepancies and aggregate agreement separately. Justify tolerances without treating the five previously scored test events as blind validation.

4. **Must fix — account for the right denominator and attrition.** There are 116 omitted *registry rows*, but **104 omitted headline events**, assuming the stated join is correct. Also, 6.664 is not an additive contribution to 7.312: the subset contributes `132.613/√500 = 5.93` headline Z units; the rest contribute about 1.38, or about 3.03 standardized on their own. Publish a one-to-one disposition of all 500 published included rows, with sums by exclusion reason. Keep reproduction eligibility independent of control availability. For inference, show results on all evaluable events, all evaluable controls, **and exactly the same evaluable pairs**. Predefine a coverage requirement or an “insufficient data” outcome; otherwise even a small, selectively surviving subset can earn a strong label.

5. **Should fix — audit event identity, not just event number.** A date within one day is not proof of a match; a date further away is not proof of a typo. Check titles, times, numbering runs and multi-date entries, with adjudications based on metadata rather than which score fits. For blocking controls and training data, cover the plausible **published as well as registry** dates of unresolved events. Dropping a disputed event from the test while blocking only its possibly incorrect date does not keep it out of the controls.

### Controls, scaling and the null distribution

6. **Must fix — use a common, out-of-sample scale for each comparison.** The event's reference data include its matched control; the control's reference data do not. The two can therefore have different denominators, pass different eggs across the 86,400-trial threshold, or differ in evaluability. For example, in a year with one event and a control touching one UTC day, removing the control's seconds leaves **no** egg with 86,400 reference trials. Moreover, controls help set *one another's* scales: leave-one-window-out is not independent validation of the combined control score. A shared scale error of just 0.5% in the expected network mean square shifts a full-day score by roughly `0.005√(86400/2) ≈ 1`. Use a predetermined disjoint set of ordinary archive days for training, or hold out whole date blocks, with each event–control pair using the same training data and scale eligibility. Report training dates, counts and egg losses. A collection of control-touched days is neither a full year nor an egg's full historical database.

7. **Must fix — the week-shift comparison is directional.** Of 396 controls, 366 are earlier than the event. A secular change in device composition, variance, uptime or data processing can therefore make the *expected difference* nonzero; it need not merely add noise. Whole weeks match UTC clock time and weekday, not holidays, local clock changes, maintenance or network state. Registry-order assignment also gives the first of two competing events its preferred control. Preassign balanced directions, or use both flanking weeks where available with a fixed rule for collisions. Show the before-only and after-only contrasts and actual changes in egg coverage. A weeks-long disturbance affecting both sides can hide an event contrast; a positive contrast against one set of weeks is not proof of specificity to registered times generally.

8. **Must fix — the egg veto is fitted to the outcome window.** It removes an egg for unusual mean or mean square in the very observations being tested. It can remove a genuine contribution, preferentially censor long windows, and retain the same malfunction in a short window but reject it in a long one. Applying the same code twice does not fix differential masking when coverage or signal differs. Separate independently determined device-quality flags from window-specific exclusions; at least show a fixed-mask/no-window-veto sensitivity and losses by veto reason. Also, “outside the range 55 to 145” ordinarily means `<55` or `>145`; the code rule removes **55 and 145 too**. Resolve this and count the affected records.

9. **Must fix — distinguish a descriptive Stouffer sum from a calibrated test.** Overlapping events reuse seconds, whereas controls are forced not to overlap. Even if every window Z had variance one, the variance of their sum is `n + 2Σcov`, not necessarily `n`. Shared fitted scales, persistent device states and temporal correlation in the squared network score create other dependencies. Reusing the project's combination for the **descriptive** result is appropriate; calling 3.09 a calibrated departure is not justified by that choice. Quantify overlap in seconds and in score contribution; show a preselected nonoverlapping subset. For inference use a null calculation preserving calendar clusters, overlap, fitting and masking, or limit the claim to a nominal statistic.

10. **Must fix — the sign-flip justification is false.** A positive difference of 10 and a negative difference of −1 can be equally probable; that does not make their signs exchangeable conditional on their magnitudes. Independent per-pair flips need invariance of the **joint** null distribution under those flips. Deterministic, mostly backward matching, asymmetric scaling and shared observations all undermine that assumption. This is not a randomized paired experiment. The flip test does *not* require chi-square marginals; its problem is exchangeability. Use a justified calendar/block null for the contrast, or give an effect estimate with dependence-aware uncertainty and explicitly conditional interpretation. More flips do not cure the design.

11. **Should fix — separate the two approximations for short windows.** If second-level composites really were independent standard normals, the sum of their squares would be chi-square even for a short window. The questions are (i) whether that model holds with discrete trial sums, changing egg counts, estimated scales, missingness and selection, and (ii) how well Wilson–Hilferty converts a *given* chi-square to Z. Checking four df values at unspecified quantiles answers neither. At df 2, for example, the lower 1% quantile converts to about −2.02 by Wilson–Hilferty rather than −2.326. That is **not** the expected error for the 60-second events, whose accepted df are at least 48. Use stable exact log-tail conversion as a comparator, specify handling of a zero statistic, and test over relevant df **and tails**. Show results without the under-hour events and with realistic short-window coverage, not only ideal noise.

12. **Should fix — distinguish device variance, offset and simultaneous network activity.** Mean square about 100 is not variance about an egg's empirical mean. Scaling by it does not remove a signed offset; products of offsets can contribute to the cross-egg statistic without any synchronous fluctuation. Its stated denominator is a null SD under centered, unit-variance independent eggs and appropriate independence across seconds, not an empirical standard error. Also, two eggs passing *somewhere* in the window need never report together: a passing window can be driven entirely by `N=1` seconds. Report the distribution of `N`, simultaneous coverage, and diagonal and cross-egg contributions; repeat requiring simultaneous eggs. A cross-egg excess would be informative but would not by itself identify its cause.

### Decision rules and implementation

13. **Must fix — non-rejection is being used as equivalence.** “Controls look like chance” means only that two cutoffs were not crossed. For 396 independent unit-normal scores the SD's standard error is about 0.036; the allowed range 0.75–1.30 tolerates substantial miscalibration. Mean and SD also miss tail and temporal failures. Similarly, controls above 2.58 and one-sided paired `p > 0.05` do not establish equal excesses: the contrast may be imprecise, or controls may be *larger*. Define scientifically material mean, spread and contrast margins and assess uncertainty against them, or say “not detected,” not “like chance” or “not specific.” A low event sum is not a test of agreement with the published scores for the retained events.

14. **Must fix — labels can conflict.** Controls at +3, events near zero and a large paired p satisfy both “not specific” and “no excess found.” “Paired difference near zero” in *What would count* has no threshold. Nor does a calibration failure diagnose a device artefact rather than a bad reference estimate or matching rule. Make outcomes mutually exclusive, specify insufficient-coverage cases, and keep numerical reproduction, event–control contrast and calibration as distinct conclusions. Don't count them as independent confirmations; the same scores enter all three.

15. **Should fix — specify remaining programmer choices.** In particular: whether “cleaned trials” in a reference pool means range cleaning only or also an egg check (on what window?); unique union of days/seconds versus repeated contributions from shared days; handling of zero/nonfinite scales, malformed fields and row/header length mismatches; exact duplicate-second handling when the last row has blanks; and what counts as an egg “used.” Specify Pearson versus other correlation, SD divisor, undefined correlations, pair order for seeded flips, and behavior with zero or one evaluable pair. Record intermediate counts and masks. Two programs handed the same `windows.tsv` and `blocked.tsv` cannot independently detect a shared table-parsing or interval error.
