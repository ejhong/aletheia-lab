**I would not lock this plan yet.** The strongest design choice is the single, prespecified North American target. The BP direction in the step is correct, and forward simulation through IntCal is preferable to treating an SPD trough as self-evident. The biggest problem is that the observed statistic is computed on **selected, site-binned dates**, while its null distribution and power are computed on **independent, unselected single dates**. Agreement between two implementations would not establish that this comparison is valid.

## Threats to the test

| Severity | Problem and consequence |
|---|---|
| **must fix** | **The simulation does not reproduce the unit of analysis.** A real bin can average ten assays, perhaps repeated measurements of one sample, perhaps several occupation episodes. A simulated bin is one assay with one randomly drawn error. Moreover, a site can contribute many correlated bins, and the error pool weights a ten-assay bin ten times as heavily as a one-assay bin. Matching the number of bins fixes the total SPD mass, not its temporal variance. The reference can be too narrow **or** too wide. Simulate records with a defensible site/episode structure, multiplicities and error distribution, then run the actual binning on them; alternatively simplify the observed unit to one for which a matched simulation is defensible. Do not fix the real bins at their observed times—that would condition on the signal under test. |
| **must fix** | **Selection and edges differ between worlds.** Real dates are selected on *measured radiocarbon age*; synthetic dates are selected on *true calendar age* and apparently retained even if their measured ages fail the real cut. Both are then renormalized on a third interval, the calibration grid. A broad assay with much of its likelihood outside that grid gets its remaining mass inflated. The target is well inside the grid, so a large direct edge effect is not a given, but the *trend used at the target* is fitted much closer to the selection edges. Generate over a padded calendar interval, apply the same measured-age selection, specify what count is being conditioned on, and demonstrate stability to more padding and wider input cuts. |
| **must fix** | **The null is narrower than “no sudden change.”** It is one exponential across 5,000 years. A smoothly turning trajectory, or a changing mixture of two regions with different histories, can yield a locally negative step that is rare under that exponential without any discontinuity. Conversely, fitting through a real decline can absorb part of it. Also, `ln S` is the log of a selected, calibrated, binned proxy—not the log of latent event intensity. Its low tails can have substantial leverage on the slope. Fit/check the background through the observation process; prespecify a smooth alternative with a stated timescale and a fit excluding the target neighborhood. Do not select whichever background gives the preferred verdict. |
| **must fix** | **The p-value is for a fitted reference, not a known reference.** The observed record chooses the trend and error mix and is then compared with fresh records conditional on those choices. The plus-one rank formula is fine for exchangeable records; it does not make these records exchangeable. Validate the false-positive rate of the *whole procedure*: generate independent pseudo-observed datasets, estimate their backgrounds, construct their references and apply the actual verdict. Merely refitting a trend inside a replicate without using that fit in its test changes nothing. |
| **must fix** | **Not every archaeological assay dates human activity on the IntCal20 clock.** Marine shell, consumers of aquatic food, freshwater reservoir effects, old wood, and geological or faunal contexts without secure human association can shift or create apparent activity, not just enlarge `Error`. An `Error ≤ 200` check does not catch a precisely measured reservoir age. Use the release’s material/context/quality metadata where available; prespecify a defensible atmospheric, human-associated subset and report how much is unclassifiable. Do not invent reservoir corrections from this table. Continent is also not quite hemisphere; check the geographical extent of the Asian subset. |
| **must fix** | **Sampling intensity is an unmodelled alternative explanation.** Repeated sampling of a boundary layer, intensive dating of diagnostic cultural phases, duplicate imports, changing preservation or access to sites, and one heavily studied subregion can all change the step. Averaging within a site-age bin does not remove preferential selection of *which sites and levels get dated*. Audit concentration and duplication. Even after that, scope the inference to the dated record: these public files alone cannot separate a change in people from a change in the probability that their traces were preserved, sampled and published. |
| **should fix** | **Fixed radiocarbon bins interact with the calibration curve.** Ages 10,999 and 11,000 are similar but in different bins; 11,000 and 11,199 can be in the same one. Two hundred radiocarbon years do not represent a constant calendar duration. A densely dated site can saturate at one unit of SPD mass per occupied bin, making a halving of underlying activity quite unlike a halving of bins. Test bin origin as well as width, and a genuinely different site-weighting scheme. Audit both false mergers (generic site names) and splits (the same site with and without an ID). |
| **should fix** | **Calibration is modelled only marginally.** The stated likelihood is reasonable for a single atmospheric date with Gaussian errors and a flat calendar prior. Adding lab noise in generation and using lab error in calibration is not, by itself, double-counting. But a separate independent `σ(t)` draw for every assay is not a realization of one uncertain curve shared by contemporaneous samples and regions. The three IntCal columns do not supply a joint curve covariance. At least compare generation on the mean curve with the proposed marginal-noise simulation and state the limitation. Plot the *forward-simulated SPD reference* at the onset: the latent exponential alone does not show the expected calibration trough or peak. |

## Decision rules

- **must fix — Non-detection plus 80% power is not “no drop.”** Even under the exact planted alternative, a test with 80% power misses one record in five. More importantly, this rule treats a near-significant negative step and a large positive step identically, though they are different evidence against a halving. Report non-detection and design sensitivity separately. If a verdict *against a halving* matters, invert simulations across crash multipliers, or directly test whether the observed step is **too high** to be compatible with the specified halving. Give a bound on the effect under the observation model. This still would not establish “no drop.”
- **must fix — Power is not power for the stated decision.** Detection requires **both** `D < 0` and `(k+1)/1001 ≤ .01`, i.e. at most nine null steps at or below the observed step. An interpolated “1st percentile” need not give that rank cutoff, and the power rule drops the sign requirement. This matters if the local null itself predicts an increase. Apply the identical decision function to every planted record. Label the alternative accurately: a 300-year rectangular reduction in *latent sampling intensity*, conditional on total count, at exactly the chosen onset—not necessarily a 50% loss of people.
- **must fix — A significant step is not a test of abruptness or of a crash-sized effect.** A gradual decline across the two windows can give the same means as an abrupt loss; an arbitrarily small decline can pass with sufficient precision. Conversely, a severe but short or displaced decline can fail. Report the observed post/pre ratio, its expected value under the reference, and effect uncertainty, not just a z-score. “Unusually low post/pre activity at the specified boundary” is a supportable detection claim; “population crashed at that moment” is not.
- **should fix — Monte Carlo error is large at both verdict boundaries.** At a tail probability of .01, 1,000 simulations give a Monte Carlo SE of about .003; at power .80, 300 give an SE of .023 **before** uncertainty in the shared null cutoff. The plus-one p-value is not wrong because 1,000 is small, but seed-dependent changes of verdict are quite plausible. Increase draws for the primary target, report Monte Carlo intervals, and prespecify what happens if uncertainty straddles a decision threshold. Window masses can be cached; not every diagnostic plot needs the same simulation budget.
- **should fix — Separate the primary, geographic and scan claims.** No 89-time correction is needed for a genuinely prespecified North American target. But “at least one of Europe and Asia” gives two opportunities for secondary confirmation: conditional on a genuine North American effect and two independent unaffected secondary regions, the chance of one nominal 1% hit is about 2%, not 1%. Shared calibration artefacts also limit claims of independent replication. Specify the error criterion for the geographic claim and avoid equating a pooled continent hit with a continent-wide decline. Adjacent scan windows overlap heavily; target rank and the five extrema are descriptive, not extra p-values. If claiming exceptional timing anywhere in the scan, compare against *whole simulated scans*.

## Reproducibility gaps

| Severity | What two competent programmers could do differently |
|---|---|
| **must fix** | **Input and identity.** Pin file hashes/commit and the actual IntCal download. Specify finite-value checks, sentinel strings and IDs, exact continent labels, ID parsing including leading zeros, and separate namespaces for ID keys and fallback names. State how duplicated lab determinations, conflicting rows, and sites represented by both keyed and unkeyed rows are handled. Publish intermediate counts after each operation. |
| **should fix** | **“The same” in the sensitivities.** Does excluding large errors rebuild bins, trend, error pool, null and power? When the target or window moves, does the planted crash move or change duration? Is power recalculated for that detector against the *original* crash or a different crash? These answer different questions. Specify what is rebuilt, what is held fixed and whether any sensitivity can change the verbal conclusion. |
| **should fix** | **Numerics and scaling.** Specify stable likelihood normalization, handling of zero SPD/window mass or zero simulation SD, grid endpoint inclusion for fitting, SD divisor, tie ranks, and scaling of the plotted reference (unit density versus total bin mass per grid point). Require sorted calendar coordinates for interpolation. Do not let an undocumented epsilon determine a log-trend fit. |
| **should fix** | **“Agree closely” and “loosely” are not replication criteria.** Require exact agreement on row selection, bin membership and counts, stated numerical tolerances on deterministic outputs, and a shared small fixture of latent ages, errors and noise draws to test the forward pipeline itself. For independent random runs, specify tolerances based on Monte Carlo uncertainty; one seed plus region order does not specify the allocation of draws across nulls, alternatives and sensitivities. |

## Missing checks, in priority order

1. **must fix — End-to-end size and power, not an “unremarkable p-value” unit test.** Measure rejection frequencies with uncertainty under a known matched null, then under plausible no-crash stress cases with clustered sites, heterogeneous errors, selection edges and smooth curvature. Apply the exact sign-and-rank decision. A correct null test should *sometimes* give a remarkable p-value; a planted event should not be required to pass in a region with little information.
2. **must fix — Audit who contributes the target-window mass.** For each side report dates, identifiable sites, bins, unmatched IDs, bin-size distribution, material/unknown fractions, and share of mass from the largest sites and available source/geographic strata. Check repeated lab codes where present; identical ages alone are not proof of duplication. Repeat leaving out dominant sites or sources and on the defensible terrestrial subset. Region-wide totals disguise whether a verdict comes from two excavations.
3. **should fix — Stress the observation and background models.** In priority to listing the five most extreme years, show observed versus simulated SPDs outside the target, results with wider age/grid support, alternative site binning, and a prespecified alternative smooth background. A reference that systematically misses the flanks is a poor yardstick even if its target p-value is very small.
4. **should fix — Measure temporal resolution at the actual onset.** Inject crashes with displaced starts and different durations, and test them with the **fixed primary detector**. Include a gradual decline of comparable total magnitude. Moving the detector and the injected event together does not measure sensitivity to misalignment. The planned 30% injection addresses magnitude only; it does not support the statement that power figures cover shorter crashes.
5. **should fix — Use a calibration fixture with a plateau or reversal.** “A single date calibrates to the right calendar age” is ill-defined for a multimodal calibration. Compare the full normalized grid weights and the two window masses with an independently calculated example, including a broad-error date and one near an edge.

## Literature and factual framing

- **should fix — Earlier tests (moderate-to-high confidence on the qualitative distinction; not on exact counts).** Buchanan, Collard and Edinborough (2008) is fairly summarized as finding no evidence for the proposed North American bottleneck, not as establishing no decline. Anderson et al. (2011) frame their result as *possible population decline/settlement reorganization* in the early Younger Dryas, using multiple lines of evidence. I would not describe it as the opposite result on the same precisely timed radiocarbon test. Read both and distinguish geography, age filters, evidence and timing before claiming a larger retest. The worldwide database total is irrelevant to that comparison; even the disclosed **broader-cut** North American count is only 2,562 before binning.
- **should fix — Ice onset versus proposed impact age (high confidence).** The conversion of 12,896 years before 2000 to 12,846 BP is correct. An ice-core event-stratigraphic age is not an exact, universally synchronous archaeological boundary or a measured impact date. For example, Kennett et al. (2015, *PNAS*) report a proposed boundary interval of 12,835–12,735 cal BP. State which prediction is anchored to which clock; an offset of this scale matters for 300-year windows.
- **should fix — Strength of the hypothesis claim (high confidence).** A simultaneous demographic halving for three centuries is a useful benchmark, not a quantitative prediction established here for the impact literature. Nor are “cosmic devastation” and “little loss” exhaustive alternatives. Distinguish testing a proposed demographic consequence from testing the impact mechanism.
- **minor — Database and genetics wording (high confidence on the qualifications).** Distinguish the Bird et al. publication from the particular scrubbed release and verify its count, licence and any “largest” superlative for that version. The categorical statement about DNA resolution is also too broad: many inferred effective-population histories are strongly smoothed, but resolution depends on sampling and method. Genetics can simply be out of scope.
