**Must fix — The plan can test a differential allele-sharing signal, but its headline claims are stronger than its tests.** A positive primary statistic is interpretable. It is not, by itself, identification of an ancestry source. The two panels are not donor replications, and the ancient decision statistic is not a bias-corrected version of the primary test. These distinctions matter even if every line of code is correct.

### Statistics and interpretation

1. **Must fix — The primary sign is right; the L2 interpretation is reversed.** Write \(s(W,O)=(p_W-p_O)(p_X-p_R)\) at a SNP. Its mean is the specified \(f_4(O,W;R,X)\). Thus positive P means that the Amazon-minus-Reference contrast is aligned with Onge-minus-Mbuti. But **positive** \(f_4(\mathrm{ONG},\mathrm{Papuan};R,X)\) favors **Papuan relative to Onge**, and negative favors Onge. A toy SNP with Papuan and X at 1 and Onge and R at 0 should be in the sign tests. For every comparison to a published \(D\), give the paper's ordered quartet and numerator convention; the sign of “ABBA minus BABA” is not universal.

2. **Must fix — N3 equals N1 minus N2 only on the same SNPs with the same weights.** N1 requires Mbuti and Onge, N2 requires Mbuti and Han, and N3 does not even require Mbuti. The plan gives them different masks. Compute the three on the five-way intersection as an explicit identity check. There is a second useful identity on a common mask: N4 equals N3 for modern Amazon minus N3 for G. Use a paired block error for a difference, not a difference of Z-scores.

3. **Must fix — Even with exact algebra, reference bias does not cancel in general.** If the observed frequency in G is \(p_G+e\), the bias term in N3 is the SNP mean of \((p_{\rm ONG}-p_{\rm Han})e\). There is no reason for that to vanish: preferential calling of the mapping-reference allele is allele- and site-dependent. Capture, mapping, damage and contaminant ancestry need not affect Han and Onge contrasts equally. Conversely, a *real* difference in Han-related ancestry between G and Mixe can change N2 and N3. Neither a null N2 nor agreement on transversions establishes absence of bias; contamination need not push N3 down. **N3 tests a different biological contrast**. First establish how well it detects the modern signal on ancient-callable sites before making it the ancient classifier.

4. **Must fix — L does not identify a closest source.** L1 positive is differential affinity to Tianyuan *relative to Mbuti*, not evidence that the source was Tianyuan-like rather than Oceanian-related. L3 positive favors Onge over Han in this contrast; it does not exclude other East Eurasian affinities. L4 needs an interpretation and reporting rule too. A nonsignificant L2 is not evidence of equidistance; give a confidence interval and, if equality matters, a meaningful equivalence margin. Even a precise Papuan-favoring L2 would not date contact or distinguish recent Pacific contact from ancient structure. The Han control is a diagnostic, not a theoretical zero across all American population histories.

Individuals tested one at a time are not a substitute for deletions: one can drive the pooled estimate while none is individually significant. Report when too few donors remain. Separately repeat key ancient comparisons by actual assay, QC status, version and transversions, on matched masks when comparing effects. 5. **Should fix — Test ascertainment and callability, not just transitions.** These are genomes projected onto the ascertained 1240K targets, not an unascertained genome-wide sample. ### Decision rules

- **Must fix — P is a detection rule, not a replication rule.** \(Z_A=3.1,\ Z_B=0.01\) passes, including when both estimates depend on the same donors and the same Onge calls. Conversely two positive estimates of 1.9 fail, regardless of their intervals. Predeclare one primary comparison and describe support from the other as a correlated sensitivity test, or require actual support in a prespecified held-out donor comparison. “Did not meet the detection criterion” is fair; “against” a signal of the published size requires an interval or power criterion on a comparable effect scale. Large negative estimates and two imprecise zeros should not receive the same interpretation.

- **Must fix — Bind the headline and technical gates to the evidence they qualify.** As written, one panel can supply the high P and the other the “partly robust.” There isn't even a unique “panel that replicates” if both P's are high. Specify which *same* panel must supply P, R and satisfactory diagnostics; what happens when the sole high P has a reference problem; and what “near zero” means. Reject the blanket provisos that P/R are unaffected by technical failures and that N2 near zero rescues N. A useful technical gate asks whether a matched technical or reference shift is large enough to change the biological conclusion.

- **Should fix — R's 90% and median-Z thresholds have no clear robustness interpretation.** Many cells reuse donors, correlated proxies and nested SNP masks. One problematic contrast is counted repeatedly; an underpowered but consistent transversion analysis is penalized for losing SNPs. Conversely, a substantial shift in magnitude can pass if signs stay positive. Report robustness by perturbation axis, including both target populations separately, with effects and paired differences on matched sites. If retaining the thresholds, call them descriptive scores, not accumulated replication evidence.

- **Must fix — S ranks precision as well as affinity.** A precisely estimated small effect can outrank a larger one in a two-person group. World labels also differ in pipeline, missingness and admixture; splitting one lineage into several correlated labels changes the top ten. Specify exactly which labels constitute the five, confirm they are in W in the required form, and state how many Papuan labels count. For inference, preselect plausible competing proxies, especially East/Southeast Asians, and test *direct* contrasts such as \(f_4(W_{\rm competitor},W_{\rm candidate};R,X)\) on matched SNPs, with uncertainty and multiplicity accounted for. A world rank is a useful plot, not a specificity test. Failure to put four labels in the top ten does not establish “not specific.”

- **Must fix — The ancient multiplicity calculation is reasonable for a narrower claim than the plan makes.** \(3.15\) is approximately the one-sided Bonferroni cutoff for **60 fixed N3 tests** at familywise 5%; dependence among sets does not invalidate that bound. It does not also cover the N4 decisions or the special search over Sumidouro5 versions and possibly proxies. Specify the families, which claims are confirmatory, the one primary Sumidouro version/proxy/test matching the publication, and how a failed-QC or discordant version is handled. “Either version significant” gives two chances; “both below 2” is not evidence of nonreplication at adequate power. Also distinguish an EARLY cell (≥8,200 BP) from the ≥9,000 BP individual screen when dating a detection. The oldest detected specimen is not an arrival date.

- **Should fix — Separate “positive,” “smaller than Amazon,” and “absent.”** G can be detectably positive on N3 *and* detectably smaller than Amazon on N4; requiring N3 < 2 for “lacks the Amazonian level” can withhold the latter conclusion precisely when G is well measured. Report both intervals. If “Amazonian strength” means a material fraction of the modern effect, define that margin on matched sites. A statement about excluding such an effect is different from a failure to detect zero departure. There is no universal conversion from 1–2% ancestry to an expected N3 or its SE.

Two programs agreeing on the same reversed interpretation or the same inappropriate mask does not validate the inference.
