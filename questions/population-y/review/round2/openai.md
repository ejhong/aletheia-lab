**The sign of the primary statistic is right.** The biggest risk is not a reversed quartet. It is finding a real, precisely estimated difference between the *sampled* Mixe and Amazonians, then using controls that cannot establish what caused that difference. The biggest internal inconsistency is that the ancient “falls short” decision relies on the very ancient–modern comparison the plan says is unsafe.

I would keep \(P_A\) as a primary test of a named quartet. I would not lock the “clean,” “specific,” or ancient “falls short” conclusions as written. This is a review of the specification, not a finding about the genotypes.

## Modern signal and its interpretation

1. **Must fix — The admixture screen selects on a statistic correlated with the outcome.** For instance, removing a Mixe donor because of \(f_4(\mathrm{Mbuti},\mathrm{French};U,i)\) changes a population in \(P_A\), using the same SNPs, Mbuti, and much of the same American contrast. The jackknife on the surviving donors treats that choice as fixed. Moreover, \(U\) is not an unadmixed standard: ancestry shared throughout \(U\) is largely invisible to this screen, while genuine differences in ancient Native ancestry can trigger it. This can mask or create an excess; there is no guaranteed conservative direction. At minimum lock the **unscreened** primary, anchor and held-out comparisons alongside the screened ones, with a decision for disagreement. Prefer exclusions fixed by independent metadata for the main comparison. If genotype-based selection is essential, test how much selection on one set of chromosomes changes the result on the others. Check residual African as well as European affinity in the final contrast.

2. **Must fix — T3 is a conditional mixture calculation, not a bound on admixture.** Write \(R=(1-\alpha)R_0+\alpha F\), with \(X=\mathrm{Amazon}\), \(F=\mathrm{French}\). On identical SNPs,
   \[
   P_{\rm obs}=(1-\alpha)f_4(M,\mathrm{Papuan};R_0,X)
               +\alpha f_4(M,\mathrm{Papuan};F,X).
   \]
   Thus the **sign** of the proposed induced term is sensible for French admixture in the reference. But the proposed estimator of \(\alpha\) also needs \(f_4(M,F;X,R_0)=0\), an appropriate French source, and effectively no competing ancestry differences. None is established by \(|Z(T3)|<3\). There is also a finite-sample error: the *same estimated French frequency occurs twice* in the denominator \(f_4(M,F;X,F)\), contributing a positive sampling-variance term. This matters particularly with three French donors. Use a within-population sampling correction or distinct French donors across slots, a common mask for the entire calculation, and joint block uncertainty. A negative estimate, a fraction with \(P\) near zero, or a very wide interval is not a “clean” pass. Report this as a sensitivity scenario, not the maximum share admixture could explain.

3. **Must fix — “Specific” conflates a difference with the absence of another effect.** On a common mask, \(L3=P-\text{Han control}\) exactly. A positive \(L3\) is useful evidence for *more Papuan than Han differential affinity*. It can coexist with a substantial positive Han effect; a noisy Han estimate makes the \(|Z|<3\) gate **easier** to pass. One Han group cannot represent all East Asian ancestry, either. If absence of a meaningful general pull matters, specify a meaningful margin and require an interval that excludes effects beyond it. Otherwise report the differential and the control separately. Use common SNPs for each decomposition: the analogous identities are \(L2=P-P_{\rm ONG}\) and \(L4=P-L1\). This is particularly important for Tianyuan. A significant L1 on Tianyuan’s mask and a significant \(P\) on the full panel need not describe the same SNPs. Also, an L2 interval divided by the point estimate of \(P\) is not an interval for a ratio.

4. **Should fix — Change the reference without simultaneously changing everything else.** \(P_A\) versus \(P_B\) changes Mixe to Pima, pipelines, people, and population weights. A disagreement cannot be attributed to any one of these. The grid contains ingredients for a better check: compute the *pooled* Amazon statistic with Mixe and Pima in A on common SNPs, and directly estimate the reference contrast with its paired SE. Do the analogous pipeline comparison using matched Pima and Amazonian people where possible. Show the held-out result with kinship to panel-A donors, not just nonidentity. A person held out from A can be a close relative of a person in A. A positive held-out result is useful; it is not an independent population replication.

5. **Should fix — Neither the grid nor leave-one-out establishes population-level robustness.** A 90% sign score allows an entire important subset of comparisons to fail; the score can change just by adding near-duplicate choices to an axis. Median \(Z\) mixes affinity with coverage and precision. Conversely, *every* leave-one-out \(Z\ge2\) can fail when all estimates are nearly identical, just because dropping one of three reference donors increases the SE. Report effect sizes and changes from the full estimate, particularly separately for Suruí and Karitiana, and give an equal-population-weight pool beside the donor-weight pool. Extend the relatedness sensitivity to A and across group labels where feasible. The block SE measures genomic uncertainty conditional on these donors; it does not sample new communities. Finally, changing SNPs and quartet members tests robustness of **one allele-frequency method**; \(D\) is a normalization of the same numerator, not an independent method.

6. **Should fix — Ascertainment controls do not undo ascertainment.** Polymorphism in B.Yoruba *within 1240K* does not make those sites a Yoruba-ascertained random sample of the genome. In some grid cells it also conditions on the very donors used as outgroup. Transversions address a different problem; leaving out a Karitiana donor does not remove SNPs discovered using Karitiana. Report counts, effects and SEs by ascertainment subset **if ascertainment can be identified from the named files**; otherwise say explicitly that it cannot be isolated. The Suruí-only contrast and a Yoruba-polymorphic sensitivity using disjoint ascertainment and outgroup donors are useful limited checks. Neither converts the anchor into a whole-genome replication.

7. **Must fix — The headline and published-size rule overreach.** \(Z<2\) includes both a tightly estimated reverse effect and an uninformative interval extending well past the published effect. “Not detected by \(P_A\) on these SNPs” covers both; “not detected in public genomes” does not, particularly if other public-data contrasts are positive. There is also no headline for \(2\le Z<3\), or precedence rule for a “doubtful” primary set. The smallest published **point estimate** from a different panel is not a lower bound on the true published effect. \(D\)'s denominator as well as its numerator depends on SNP selection. Use the anchor interval to state which **1240K quartet effects** are compatible, not to reject an array or sequencing effect of “published size” without calibration. Neither \(D=0.02\) nor \(f_4\) means 2% ancestry.

## Technical and ancient comparisons

8. **Must fix — T1 and T2 are not necessarily centered on zero as implemented for pools.** Suppose one person has a genotype only in the capture pool at a SNP, and a different person only in the shotgun pool. The product now contains a *between-person* difference despite the nominal pairing. Use people called in **both** versions at each SNP and form the within-person differences before averaging. Show per-person, per-population, and pooled estimates: opposite biases in Pima and Suruí could cancel in the pool yet reinforce in the primary contrast. Conversely, a real pipeline offset common to both American groups need not bias their difference. Compare the **magnitude and interval of the bias in the relevant contrast** with \(P\), not just its \(Z\). Nonsignificant pooled T tests are not validation, particularly for eight ancient pairs.

9. **Must fix — K1/K3 break the design restriction, and a T2 failure does not invalidate just the “paper-form” statistics.** In K, \(G\) can mix capture and shotgun, and is compared directly to modern diploid A.Amazon. A common mask equalizes loci, not reference-allele preference, mapping, contamination, damage, or genotype calling. Both K statistics share the same ancient–modern difference; a bias projecting on Papuan versus Han can pass both gates. At least split K by type, check transversions, use paired versions to estimate shifts in **K itself**, and mask/pseudo-haploidize modern donors for comparison. Those operations still cannot reproduce ancient reference bias. Unless residual bias can be bounded relative to the claimed deficit, make “falls short” descriptive, not a decision. This applies to the coastal conclusion too.

10. **Must fix — Type matching alone does not make the ancient baseline comparable.** A suffix is not evidence of identical enrichment, library treatment, read processing, or absence of merged data. Cross-tabulate suffix against the AADR data-source/type and quality annotations; flag discordances. At a SNP a large pool may be represented by one high-coverage site or family; at the next SNP it may represent a different region or century. Also \(B=\mathrm{BASE}_t\setminus G\) changes with \(G\): a MESO result and a South American result need not even have the same comparator. Report contributing people/sites and coverage, the composition and date range of each baseline, and repeat high-leverage findings with a site left out and a narrower, temporally comparable baseline where possible. A positive result versus this composite is not a ranking of ancient groups on one common scale. The coast-versus-north test is not coast-versus-inland; the latter contrast is feasible for some periods and more relevant to geographic specificity.

11. **Should fix — The ancient gates have no general bias-cancellation property.** Adding French contamination to \(G\) moves M1 toward \(f_4(M,\mathrm{Papuan};B,F)\), not necessarily downward. Similarly, the effect of East Asian ancestry depends on the source and baseline. Reference bias need not produce significant MF. A positive transversion M1 might be arbitrarily small and noisy. Conversely, the conjunction can miss a genuine M1 excess that is partly shared with Han. Name the conjunction for what it selects: a Papuan-over-baseline result *also differential to Han*, subject to QC. Report the component estimates, their masks, and how much bias the controls could have missed. Using public allele frequencies, one can inject small French or Han mixtures into the actual contrasts to check the asserted directions and sensitivity without claiming to simulate ancient DNA.

12. **Should fix — Power is assessed for a different test from the ancient decision.** The downsample experiment uses Mixe, one modern Amazonian, and one \(f_4\). The decision uses an ancient baseline, a multiplicity-adjusted threshold, M3, MF, and a transversion gate. It is possible to recover the anchor in downsampling and have almost no chance of passing “stands out.” Report the minimum detectable M1 and K1 effects given each set’s SE and threshold. For leading individual claims, repeat masking and allele draws on the actual decision masks and propagate the joint block variation of the gates. Distinguish loss of power from systematic bias, which this exercise cannot estimate. Multiple versions of one early person should be sensitivities around one preselected eligible version, not extra chances to establish early presence.

13. **Should fix — The world ranking is not a like-for-like specificity control.** Different \(W\)'s change the SNP mask, number of donors, population history, and SE. A high rank by \(Z\) can mean a better measured contrast, not a larger affinity. Check that the named labels actually qualify for W, show SNP counts and intervals, and compare key Australasian and East Asian proxies on common masks. Keep the ranking descriptive as proposed.

## Reproducibility and decision details

14. **Must fix — Resolve inconsistent membership after screening.** “Dropped from every set” conflicts with restoring an unscreened set of size less than two. Can a restored Suruí donor contribute to A.Surui but not A.Amazon? Is a person failing in A removed in B? Is leave-one-out run on fixed survivors or is the screen rerun? Which results are barred, rather than merely marked doubtful? Lock a post-screen membership algorithm and emit the realized membership of *every* affected set. Also specify ties for highest-SNP-count versions and the mapping of genetic IDs to duplicate people. A locked TSV resolves the primary program’s initial choices but not independent reconstruction or these later decisions.

15. **Must fix — Specify the exact ancient rejection family.** “Corrected for the number” could mean Bonferroni, Šidák, or a step-down procedure; they need not agree, especially with nested sets. State the formula, which tests enter the count, and what happens to skipped baselines, non-Pass versions, zero SEs, and overlapping versions. ANC.NCA against its own complement has no baseline. Say explicitly whether *only* all-SNP **Papuan** M1 can trigger a decision or ONG is an alternative trigger. Define eligibility independently of the resulting \(Z\)'s. Separate families for “stands out” and “falls short” are defensible, but are not a single 5% error guarantee across both claims.

16. **Must fix — Validate the block map and SEs on the actual files.** I do not see a sign or linear-jackknife algebra error: for the SNP mean the stated pseudovalue \(\tau_j\) reduces to \(s_j/m_j\) and \(\theta_J=\theta\). That gives a good unit test. The greater risk is that column 3 has unexpected units, long constant runs, missing values, or implausible chromosome spans. Emit chromosome spans, occupied block counts, largest block weight, SNP counts and leave-one-chromosome influence. Define handling of fewer than two blocks, zero SE, and zero full or delete-block \(D\) denominators. Repeat key SEs with larger blocks. For composite ratios, delete the **same block from all terms**. Make an independent raw-quartet check of SNP count, estimate **and SE** a priority, not just agreement of two translations of the same formula.

17. **Should fix — Remaining implementation choices can change results near thresholds.** Specify the KING-robust formula, SNP filters, treatment of missing calls and any LD pruning; per-SNP intersection and weighting of paired versions; RNG initialization, donor/SNP order and repeated draws; treatment of non-ACGT allele codes; and deterministic coastline interpolation/ties near 75 km. Distinguish 8,200 BP “EARLY cell” from the 9,000 BP early-individual criterion in every early-presence statement. For replication define “1%” relative to what, require mask/block counts to agree, and include the **anchor \(D\), K, and grid decision outputs**. As written two programs can satisfy the replication rule and disagree on a headline or ancient deficit.

## Literature assessment

- **Should fix — high confidence:** Yang et al.'s relevant observation is *differential Tianyuan allele sharing among Native Americans*. “Tianyuan shares it” can be read as assigning the same Population Y ancestry component to Tianyuan. The former is testable here; the latter does not follow. Likewise Onge, Papuan and Australian are distinct proxies, not samples of the proposed source.
- **Should fix — moderate to high confidence:** Raghavan et al. should not be folded into a consensus on arrival with initial peopling. They discussed later/indirect gene flow in explaining Australo-Melanesian affinities. A related allele-sharing observation is not agreement on its history.
- **Should fix — high confidence about the methodological distinction:** Skoglund's published sequencing and array results should be tied to their exact donors, SNP sets, quartet order and \(D\) convention. Sharing SGDP people does not by itself establish “the same genomes” in the sense of identical quartet membership, calls and sites. I see no basis here to dispute the quoted numeric values, but the proposed anchor is a **reanalysis on 1240K**, not a numerical reproduction requirement.
- **Minor — high confidence:** Loss of significance after removing relatives in Castro e Silva et al. is not evidence that the point estimate vanished; report effect changes separately if using this as motivation. Similarly, Posth et al.'s ancient nondetections are not a refutation of their present-day result. The plan mostly respects this distinction.
- **Should fix — verification needed, not an asserted factual error:** Check the Sumidouro5 version, quartet, direction, and Panama individual against the primary Moreno-Mayar paper and the **final** Campelo dos Santos article. Changing a comparator can change the biological contrast, not merely the “form” of a test. I cannot independently substantiate the exact seven-group count or ancient coverage claimed for the 2026 citation here; verify those against the article/supplement before using them as benchmarks. Controlled genotype access does not prevent checking a published table.

**Three changes I would make first:**
1. Lock an unscreened modern comparison and paired reference sensitivities; replace the T3 “clean” pass with an assumption-explicit estimate and interval.
2. Remove the confirmatory ancient “falls short” label unless matched-version and type-specific checks bound bias in K itself.
3. Lock and independently test the masks, map, block SEs, and decision families before computing any headline.
