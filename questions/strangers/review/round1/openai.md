**Must fix — The primary test and the proposed negative conclusion are about different things.** Step B asks whether a person has *more proxy affinity than their selected neighbours*. If a Greenland burial and all 20 neighbours are Norse, \(p_X-p_P\) is zero in expectation, and every \(E_R\) is zero in expectation. That is the correct behaviour of the statistic, not a failed scan. The same counterexample applies to a settlement with substantial shared admixture. Conversely, an unadmixed person can be an outlier relative to a poorly matched pool. I would keep the outlier question, but not interpret its answer as an inventory of everyone with ancestry from across an ocean. Even a genuine ancestry outlier does not establish when, by which route, or in which direction a crossing occurred.

## 1. Statistics and sources of error

- **Must fix — The displayed signs are not reversed; the interpretation of Step A is the problem.** On the same SNPs, the ordinary product for \(f_4(Mbuti,R;P,X)\) is \((p_R-p_{Mbuti})(p_X-p_P)\), as intended. Likewise, D1 points toward NA relative to Han, and D2 toward NA relative to MA1. But *not Native American* is not the negative of either contrast. For a concrete counterexample, an American-side burial with \(p_X=p_{Mbuti}\) has D1 = D2 = 0, not a negative signal, even at 100% foreign ancestry. Elsewhere, passing both contrasts establishes two relative affinities, not descent from a population in the Americas. Step A needs its own validated scope and power statement; it is not a substitute for B in precisely the sparse places where B is unavailable.

- **Must fix — Centring changes the estimand and introduces an allele-coding dependency.** Write the two allele-frequency differences as \(a_i,b_i\). Flipping the counted allele at a subset of SNPs leaves every \(a_i b_i\) unchanged, but need not leave \(\bar a\bar b\) unchanged. Thus two correctly harmonised representations of the same genotypes give identical conventional \(f_4\)'s and potentially different *centred* ones. Centring removes constant offsets on a fixed mask; it does not remove locus-, ancestry-, or protocol-dependent reference bias. Specify the actual allele orientation in the files, run conventional \(f_4\) alongside it, and audit disagreements by type and SNP subset. Conventional \(f_4\) is a comparator, not itself a cure for biased calls.

- **Must fix — Two definitions of \(S\) are not generally equal.** With one common SNP mask and common weights, the centred \(S\) is the covariance of \(p_X-p_P\) with \((p_{NA}-p_{Mbuti})-\sum_R u_R(p_R-p_{Mbuti})\). With “every set in the statistic has data,” A, \(B_{MA1}\), and \(B_{Han}\) can each have a different mask and different centring means. Their regression residual is then not the advertised single covariance. Choose one definition for training, scoring, and jackknifing. Report how many SNPs and informative blocks survive it: 100,000 calls in X is not 100,000 overlapping calls with MA1, the references, and P.

- **Must fix — The regression is a predictor, not an identification of Native American ancestry.** Its coefficients describe the variation common in the training rows. A rare Siberian lineage can leave a positive residual without American back-migration; real American-related variation in training can be fitted away. Both predictor and response have correlated measurement error because they share X, P, and Mbuti. A large site can enter repeatedly as targets and neighbours. Trimming extremes does not solve any of these problems. Check fit, rank, coefficient stability, and held-out residuals by region, site, and data type. Use training independent of the target/site or propagate fitting uncertainty through the uncertainty calculation; a jackknife with \(u\) held fixed on its own training data does not do that.

- **Must fix — Reference labels are not unadmixed ancestry components.** The modern NA groups differ in drift, composition, and potentially recent admixture. Recent European affinity in NA, for example, can make an Old World contrast look more “American.” Han and MA1 are not the two literal ancestral populations. Papuan is not a general proxy for Polynesians, and a positive \(E_{Han}\) can reflect variation among Indigenous American lineages without a new Asian arrival. Test NA groups separately or leave them out in turn; document reference membership, weighting, and proxy specificity. The alternative “ancient Americans” are selected by *burial side*, so can include the very Norse or Arctic cases that show why burial side is not ancestry.

- **Must fix — Distinct names do not guarantee independent genotypes.** Check genetic IDs, individual IDs, duplicates, and annotated relatives across X, P, references, and simulation donors. In particular, check whether `MA1.SG` is also a target or a neighbour in a statistic using MA1. Self-comparison can include shared call noise as well as genuine drift. Close kin in P can hide a stranger; multiple relatives can manufacture apparent site replication. Define exclusions and rerun important results without annotated close kin and same-site pools.

- **Should fix — Matching a suffix is not matching a measurement process.** Audit suffix against the release's assay/library annotations and tabulate what is routed to A only. Capture design, shotgun calling, UDG treatment, coverage, study, and pseudo-haploid versus diploid calls can differ within a nominal type. Modern panels and ancient proxies can have different SNP ascertainment and missingness; per-site membership of P can change systematically with X's assay. Check study/type effects, matched SNP masks, and per-site numbers contributing to P and references. Apply quality checks to the *baseline and references*, not just to the person who becomes a hit.

## 2. Decision rules

- **Must fix — Define the error family and validate its null.** Bonferroni does not require independent neighbours, but it does require valid marginal tail probabilities. A block jackknife estimates genomic sampling uncertainty; it does not make poorly matched populations exchangeable or guarantee normal tails at a one-in-many-thousands threshold. The separate 5% budgets for A and B are not a 5% budget for “at least one” across the scan. Counting the elsewhere A conjunction once is not intrinsically a mistake: both components must pass. Specify the joint claim, eligible denominators and treatment of invalid tests. Examine negative-control tails as well as positive controls; a shuffled-genome run is a software check, not a biological null.

- **Must fix — The Greenland requirement is unfair, and the classification can discard genuine evidence.** Tabulate which known individuals actually qualify, their dates, pools, and what contrast should distinguish them. A Norse individual against a non-Norse baseline is a useful positive control; a Norse community against itself is a useful *relative-null* control. An `-o…` suffix says “outlier,” not “this particular ancestry was previously established.” Three hits in one neighbourhood might be the strongest evidence for a migrant community. Report the statistical result, prior evidence for the *same* ancestry, cluster status, and quality concerns separately. Neither a known positive nor an unresolved contaminated sample is evidence of absence.

- **Must fix — The robustness gate is incomplete and not independent confirmation.** There is no complete second-reference rule for D1/D2 or the centre-to-centre tests. For alternate-NA \(S\), is the regression refitted? For a negative hit, “at least half” needs to refer to *absolute magnitude in the original direction*. Specify what happens if only one of several triggering contrasts replicates, or there are too few transversion blocks. Half the magnitude with a huge SE is little evidence, while a good but differently drifted proxy can legitimately give less than half. Report overlap and uncertainty for both checks. Transversions reduce some damage effects; they do not eliminate contamination, ascertainment, or reference bias. Missing X-chromosome contamination estimates and good mitochondrial matches do not certify uncontaminated nuclear DNA.

- **Must fix — The centres have a distinct baseline problem.** A union of pools need not match the composition of the centre. For example, many centre members from one site can share the same 20 controls, while two distant members contribute 40 different controls. The union overweights the distant controls even if every individual is perfectly local. Removing centre members *after* pool construction can also leave few or no local controls. Compare a centre aggregate to correspondingly weighted member-specific non-centre contrasts, or demonstrate matching by site, time, type, and actual contributing members. Define the fate of members with no pool. A pooled excess does not imply all members share it: check individual contributions and leave-one-site/person-out results.

- **Should fix — Centre labels and \(Z=3.5\) do not define a historical hypothesis by themselves.** All `Egypt_…`, `Iraq_…`, or recent-enough `Peru_…` labels are not necessarily residents of early urban centres. Freeze a roster with site, period, and rationale; specify type matching for an Old World centre used “as a whole.” List the actual number of centre contrasts and the intended error criterion. A fixed 3.5 threshold is not necessarily too lenient, but it has no stated relationship to that family of tests. No significant result from two burials is not evidence against shared ancestry without a sensitivity estimate.

- **Must fix — The injections cannot support “no one ... carries more than about that share.”** They measure an *average conditional recovery rate* for two donor scenarios, using B and the initial Z gate. They omit A-only people, centres, other sources, robustness failures, and shared ancestry already in P. Per-SNP replacement is not recent admixture in chromosomal tracts and can give a different jackknife SE. A high recovery rate among 200 well-matched recipients places no upper bound on ancestry in the least informative burial. Report recovery fractions with uncertainty by direction, donor, overlap, and pool quality, preferably through the full decision pipeline. Report untested and poorly powered strata explicitly. The defensible negative is “no additional outlier meeting these criteria; here is where comparable outliers were recoverable.”

Shared descent in northeast Siberia is not itself a known case of a person carrying ancestry *from* America.
