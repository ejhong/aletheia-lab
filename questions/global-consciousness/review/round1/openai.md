**Must fix — the primary \(p\)-value is not calibrated.** Even a network of perfectly independent, stable devices need not give the reference distribution in the plan. The main problem is not the Wilson–Hilferty approximation. It is that the two windows are used to scale each other, treated as if they had known scales, and then treated as independent when convenient. The event list can be fixed without looking at a single bit and this problem will still occur.

### Statistic and sources of error

- **Must fix — reciprocal standardisation changes both variance and covariance.** An underestimated control RMS inflates *every* affected event second; that error does not average away over the event. Also, \(E[1/s^2]\ne 1/E[s^2]\), so “none on average” is not justified. For a concrete benchmark, take \(N\) independent Gaussian eggs reporting throughout two equal, long windows, with no screening. To first order the variance of each window score is \(1+1/N\), and the covariance of the two scores is \(-2/N\): the event’s marginal-variance fluctuation enters its own score positively and the control’s negatively. Thus the variance of their difference divided by \(\sqrt2\) is about \(1+3/N\), **1.6 rather than 1 at \(N=5\)**. These are illustrative calculations, not correction factors for the real masks. Use independent, sufficiently precise scale estimates or calibrate the actual coupled statistic. An event-side variance anomaly can also *depress the control score*; the latter is not an independent diagnostic.

- **Must fix — specify what “chance” means.** With \(N\) reporting eggs, \(Z^2=\sum_i z_i^2/N+2\sum_{i<j}z_i z_j/N\). The first term can rise through marginal device variance alone; the second can rise through synchrony, duplicated streams, or aligned mean offsets. RMS scaling about 100 is not centring. As a scale check, a residual 0.25% excess in the *composite* second moment throughout a full day is worth about \(0.0025\sqrt{86400/2}=0.52\) event-score units. Distinguish “not ideal independent bits” from “unusual for this network at comparable times.” Either could be a question, but they need different reference distributions. Report the diagonal and cross-egg contributions so a reader can tell what moved.

- **Must fix — the degrees of freedom count timestamps, not independent observations.** Serial dependence in \(Z_t^2\) changes the variance of its sum; simultaneous dependence between eggs can change its expectation. Changing \(N\) is not itself fatal under an independent known-scale null, but changing device mix, uptime and shared faults can matter greatly. The cube-root transform is quite adequate at \(df\ge43{,}200\) *if* the sum really is chi-square. Even ideal 200-bit trials are discrete, not Gaussian, but that small fourth-moment discrepancy is much less concerning than untested dependence or scale error. Using an exact chi-square tail would not fix those.

- **Should fix — the egg check selects on components of the outcome.** Screening both sides symmetrically is better than screening one, but does not preserve the theoretical null distribution. A real event-related shift in mean or marginal variance can cause the affected egg to be dropped from both sides. Conversely, a short bad run, clock fault or duplicate stream can pass both whole-day checks. The nominal five-standard-error limits also assume essentially independent trials. Include cleaning in calibration and report removals by reason and by side, not just the number left.

- **Must fix — file presence is not exposure.** A partial day passes the file test; different eggs can contribute to different portions of the event and control. A quake-related outage could remove the very hours or devices of interest while leaving an “evaluable” pair. A null would then concern *observed, retained network output*, not the whole event. Freeze the joint eligibility rule and report hourly coverage, active egg identities/counts, longest gaps and asymmetric losses. Do not replace a poor-coverage control after seeing its score.

### Events, controls and decisions

- **Must fix — audit dependence between windows before using \(\sqrt n\).** The control buffer does not forbid event–event overlap, control–control overlap or control reuse. Related shocks can also share a response period without sharing a timestamp. These are possibilities to check against `events.tsv`, not claims that overlaps exist. Publish an interval-intersection and reuse table for *all* pairs; predefine clustering, exclusion or joint calibration where necessary. Two analyses of the same seconds are not two independent replications.

- **Should fix — the controls are weekday-matched but not necessarily exchangeable.** Preferring \(-7\) days systematically puts controls earlier; network drift, seasonality and roster changes need not cancel. A later control can be in the aftermath of a disaster; an unlisted major event can occur on either control. Conversely, starting at the origin hour can include up to 59 minutes before a shock and miss attention developing more than a day later. Those are defensible fixed windows, not measurements of when attention actually occurred. Check local time trends with predeclared placebo windows; do not optimise the lag on the earthquakes.

- **Must fix — remove or jointly calibrate the control-triggered switch.** A negative extreme control helps trigger the difference test *and* makes that test easier to pass. A positive extreme does the opposite. The nominal tails do not apply to this decision tree even for independent standard normal event and control aggregates. Nor can one control aggregate near zero validate a tail probability of 0.001. Choose an absolute or a matched-relative primary estimand in advance; report the other regardless. If calibration fails, “nominal inference unavailable” is preferable to switching to a second uncalibrated test.

- **Must fix — the fallback has the wrong effect-size target as well as the wrong standard error.** Even granting independent unit-variance scores, an event excess of 0.33 and control excess of zero imply a mean of \(0.33\sqrt{n/2}\) for the proposed difference, **1.78, not 2.51**, at \(n=58\). With reciprocal scaling, even the assumption about the control mean under an alternative needs checking.

- **Must fix — distinguish a benchmark from a replication prediction.** \(7.310/\sqrt{500}=0.327\) is an arithmetic mean of historical normal-equivalent scores, not an established common effect for every recipe, duration, event class and network size. For example, a common pair correlation \(\rho\) among unit-variance centred eggs gives \(E[Z_t^2]=1+(N-1)\rho\): fewer eggs can mean a smaller score for the *same physical effect*. The historical mean is itself estimated. Testing against a stipulated target of 0.33 is legitimate if labelled as such; comparing two estimated historical and modern means requires uncertainty for both. The two classes were chosen partly for good historical performance. That does not by itself invalidate a fresh-data null test, but it makes their historical subgroup means optimistic predictors.

- **Should fix — verdicts overstate what non-rejection says.** “No departure” means “no detected **positive** excess”; a strongly negative score would get the same label. “Compatible” means only “not shown smaller,” and would be awarded to an arbitrarily large excess. Report an effect estimate and interval. The quoted 28% power for “departure” is approximately right **under the stipulated independent unit-normal shift model**; the stringent threshold is not intrinsically unfair, but failure to reach it is the most likely outcome even at the benchmark. The “suggestive” tier should not become a substitute success criterion, nor should a positive class or death-threshold subset rescue a failed primary test.

### Reproducibility gaps

- **Must fix — freeze the event-builder inputs as well as its output.** Archive hashes, NOAA response including all pages, query date, event IDs, death field (direct versus total including related hazards), treatment of revised counts, duplicate records and missing/imprecise origin times. Specify half-open UTC intervals, whether the *whole* window must be in range, whether controls may fall outside it, which candidates count for the buffer, exactly how the 24-hour separation is measured, and the fate of a candidate if all four controls fail. Supply selected control intervals and exclusion reasons. Giving two programmers the same TSV tests the scorer, not independent reproduction of the selection.

- **Must fix — specify parser and paired-mask behaviour.** Define the response to repeated or changed `12` headers, duplicate egg IDs or timestamps, reordered columns across midnight, malformed/noninteger fields, missing-value codes, corrupt files and conflicts between timestamps and file dates. Fail loudly on unknown layouts. State explicitly whether the intersection of passing egg IDs is used on both sides, whether RMS uses *all* surviving egg trials or only seconds meeting \(N\ge5\), and whether **both** windows must meet the seconds criterion for either to enter either aggregate. Specify what is reported if no pairs survive. Hash the actual day files, not just the availability list.

- **Should fix — keep pre-lock validation independent of scored controls.** The proposed control-only run cannot exercise reciprocal scaling or the final joint egg mask without event data. Moreover, these particular controls later supply both denominators and a verdict trigger; inspecting them and tuning rules is not outcome-independent. Use pre-2016 or disjoint development days instead. Set numerical replication tolerances and a disagreement rule before running either implementation; agreement between programs does not validate the statistical model.

### Missing checks, highest priority first

1. **Must fix — measure end-to-end false verdict rates.** Simulate independent binomial devices with realistic rosters, gaps and repeated timestamps *where windows overlap*, running selection, QC, scaling and aggregation exactly as specified. Then stress-test stable device biases, drift, shared noise and bursts of missingness; inject both marginal and cross-egg alternatives to see what QC removes. Test rates and interval coverage with Monte Carlo uncertainty, not just whether individual scores “look normal.”
2. **Must fix — test the reference distribution on real non-event periods.** Predefine placebo schedules from the named archive, matched as far as feasible on era, season, weekday/hour, coverage and window/cluster geometry, and run the complete pipeline. Examine location, spread, tails, event–control covariance and autocorrelation of \(Z_t^2\). If used for inference, state the time-exchangeability assumptions; neither arbitrary second shuffles nor independent pair sign flips are automatically valid. Many overlapping resamples do not create many independent years of tail data. If the archive cannot support a 0.001 tail under credible assumptions, say so.
3. **Should fix — validate provenance and the reader on a published standard event.** Reconstruct egg counts, timestamps, seconds used and score for one or more pre-2016 registry windows from the same day-file product. Establish what masking or standardisation that product already contains. Quantify any mismatch; do not tune the modern analysis until a historical \(p\)-value matches.
4. **Should fix — publish influence and fault diagnostics.** Alongside coverage and the diagonal/cross-egg split, report leave-one-event/cluster and leave-one-egg influence, extreme-second contributions, and conspicuously identical streams. These can distinguish a broad excess from one faulty device pair or one exceptional hour. Keep them diagnostic rather than post-result deletion rules.
