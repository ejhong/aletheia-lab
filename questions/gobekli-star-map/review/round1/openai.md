**Must fix — The plan has three different targets:** whether the models put the claimed animals *first*; whether the claimed assignment has an unusually *low total rank*; and whether substituting the models’ rankings reproduces the paper’s *numerical odds*. These can give three different answers on the same data. The proposed headline is decided by the second test, reads like an answer to the first, and is used as evidence about the third. I would not lock the labels as written.

## Statistics and decision rules

1. **Must fix — A very significant result need not reproduce a single first choice.** Suppose every judge, on every trial, puts the same non-pillar animal first and the claimed animal second for each figure. The claimed sum is 16. An assignment can beat or tie it only by using all seconds, or by using the common first once and spending the one rank saved on at most one third. At most 65 of 19,958,400 assignments qualify: the experiment passes easily, with **zero of eight** claimed animals judged best. Report, as distinct outcomes, the per-figure top-choice frequencies, the number of claimed animals first in the consensus table, the claimed ranks versus the paper’s seven firsts and one tied second, and the aggregate assignment percentile. Decide which of these would constitute *reproduction* before judging.

2. **Must fix — Item 2 is exact for a conditional null, not “the chance of coincidence.”** Its null is: *hold these score tables fixed and assign eight distinct symbol identities uniformly to the eight figures*. The mean under that null is indeed 6.5 for complete Task A tables. But the experiment randomized **display order**, not the historical assignments, the paper’s selection of figures, or the membership of the pillar set. Enumeration gives neither uncertainty over new model calls nor the probability that the pillar does not encode a date. Even “better fit” needs qualification: the experiment defines it as a lower **sum** of ranks, allowing a gain on one figure to offset a loss on another. That is a reasonable score, not uniquely the paper’s question.

3. **Must fix — Item 3 is a formula substitution, not a valid second probability test.** In an untied table the product counts choices in which *every* row uses the claimed symbol or a better one, allowing repeated symbols. It does not count all assignments with a better total score. For the no-repeat denominator, even some choices in that product can be illegal: two rows might both improve by taking the same animal. Ties create a further failure: if all mean ranks are tied in every row, the proposed strict-better rule gives \(P+1=1\), the most impressive possible plug-in number, for a table with **no discrimination at all**. At the other extreme the doubled ratio can exceed one. Show the substitution if desired, explicitly labelled as such; do not interpret its reciprocal as odds. If reporting a count of legal assignments satisfying a row-by-row criterion, enumerate those assignments and specify how equals count.

4. **Must fix — “Do not see them” is not established by \(p>0.05\).** That result means the aggregate did not clear a particular assignment-null threshold. It can coexist with several strong matches, an unreliable judge, or opposing vendors that cancel. Conversely, making *every* vendor clear 0.01 for a positive label makes success harder when a third vendor qualifies, while the negative label has no agreement requirement. Define a substantively meaningful shortfall from the paper’s ranking if a negative claim is wanted; otherwise say “not unusually low under this null” and show effects and disagreement. Evaluate the rules on synthetic tables representing paper-like agreement, partial agreement, conflicting judges and noise. This costs no calls.

5. **Must fix — Separate identifying the pillar animals from pairing them correctly.** Eight images are the claimed set and four are distractors. A judge that generally prefers the eight because of crop style, recognizability or actual resemblance gets credit in the 12-symbol test even if it pairs them poorly. Keep that test for the paper’s 12-symbol question, but also permute **only the eight claimed symbols** (\(8!\)), and the seven remaining ones with the anchor fixed (\(7!\)). These answer “given the animals on the pillar, did the judges get *which goes with which*?” Report the pillar-versus-distractor preference separately.

6. **Should fix — Give the anchor analysis one declared role.** Conditioning on the scorpion is sensible if the question is what is identified *beyond the starting correspondence*. The all-eight result is still relevant to reproduction of Table 1. Name one primary for each stated question; do not allow either of two headline labels to stand for success on the same claim. “Carried by the scorpion alone” is also imprecise: if only its row discriminates and all other rows are flat, even a unique best scorpion gives \(p=1/12\), not \(p\le .001\). It can materially strengthen a result supported by other rows. Dropping it in analysis does **not** undo having shown the scorpion among the options on the other trials.

7. **Should fix — Treat Task B as a diagnostic, not an independent replication.** “Best of twelve animals for this figure” does not imply “best of eight figures for this animal.” Both the competitors and the direction of comparison change; the same judges and images are reused. A disagreement is informative, not necessarily an error by either the judge or the paper.

## Measurement, blinding and repetitions

8. **Must fix — Audit what the judge actually sees, including the answer key hidden in the identifiers.** `A0k ↔ S0k` encodes the answer. Neutral-looking filenames are not neutral if exposed together. Inspect a serialized request for each vendor: filenames, URLs, captions, alt text, legends, PDF remnants, image order and label-to-image binding. Check that no original table cell, neighboring symbol, rank or name survives cropping. Verify the decoder from displayed letters back to identities on a known permutation. Either leakage or a one-position binding error could dominate the entire experiment.

9. **Must fix — Verify the stimulus transformation against the table, not just the build script.** Compare each extracted photo and each black tracing side by side with the rendered source, checking aspect ratio, orientation, missing segments at stars, spurious joins, clipped appendages, white patches, padding and relative stroke width. Then inspect the *delivered* size/detail: a 512-pixel canvas does not restore detail to a 64-by-82 frog, and a service may further downsample a multi-image request. An erased fish or an artificially thick Libra could change a specific claimed result. Freeze the audited assets and hashes before calls. Taking the authors’ examples as given conditions the test on their choices; subsequent rendering does not necessarily favour them.

10. **Must fix — Recognition is neither measured reliably by the last call nor one-directional.** Recognizing a scorpion, recognizing a constellation, identifying Göbekli Tepe, and recalling the paper’s *specific assignments* are different. The full panel may cue recall absent on single trials; failure to name the source does not demonstrate absence of learned associations. Familiarity might elicit the published answer, a familiar competing constellation convention, or a learned criticism. Use fresh, stateless calls, disable retrieval/tools, and prespecify a recognition rubric separating those levels. Report recognition alongside per-vendor outcomes; do not retrospectively discard an inconvenient judge. “Not told the key” is supportable; “did not know the key” is not.

11. **Should fix — Lock the intended meaning of resemblance.** Outline, internal stick skeleton, pose, appendages, and animal identity can suggest different winners. The plan fixes the orientation of the *image*, not whether judges should mentally rotate or reflect it. Suppressing all astronomical context can help isolate shape, but can also remove information the authors used; an explicitly shape-only loss is not necessarily a failure of “perceived fit.” Publish the exact prompts, including instructions about transformations, ties and forced choices. A prespecified sensitivity using the source’s less restrictive “perceived to fit” wording, without naming any target pair, is preferable to arguing afterward about what a judge should have done.

12. **Should fix — Five shuffles are little protection against order and letter effects.** For a given query the claimed item cannot occupy twelve different positions in five trials. Using identical orders across vendors is useful for matched comparisons, but also exposes all of them to the same fortunate or unfortunate placements. Counterbalance claimed positions and competitor positions across the schedule, randomize trial sequence, use separate contexts, and include some repeated orders to distinguish stochastic changes from order changes. Report rank stability and letter/position preferences. If calls are scarce, shift effort from Task B to Task A rather than treating the many enumerated assignments as a substitute for repetitions.

13. **Should fix — Valid syntax is not judge competence or independence.** A model can emit A through L on every call and qualify. The plumbing test is useful but may not exercise the actual image count, resolution and binding. Use the production layout with an existing picture duplicated as the query and one option, at varied positions, as a basic binding/visibility control. Success does not validate archaeological judgment; failure matters. Record exact model snapshot if available, image-detail settings, sampling/reasoning limits, dates and defaults. Vendors can share training material and visual conventions; conclusions should name the tested models, not generalize to independent human observers.

## Places where two programmers could disagree

14. **Must fix — Missingness and retries.** A judge can complete 64/80 while missing *every repetition* for a particular figure. Its table, and hence the pooled permutation statistic, is then undefined. Set minimum coverage **per query and task**, and say whether failing B can disqualify A. Specify the parser, treatment of explanatory text, whether retries keep the same images/order and include the prior reply, which valid reply counts, and treatment of API failures versus malformed rankings. Keep all attempts and report missingness by query, judge and order. Dropping difficult trials or excluding an entire vendor on format grounds can select the result.

15. **Must fix — Specify the seven-item calculation fully.** Deleting the scorpion column from the finished mean table is different from removing it from each valid ranking, closing the rank gaps, and *then* averaging. In the latter version chance mean rank is 6; in the former it depends on the deleted ranks. Sorting the remaining **mean** ranks and assigning fresh ordinal ranks is a third method. The no-repeat assignment count is \(11P7=1{,}663{,}200\). For the plug-in repeat denominator, explicitly excluding scorpion gives \(11^7\); fixing one location to scorpion but allowing that symbol to recur elsewhere gives \(12^7\). These are different conditions.

16. **Must fix — Define aggregation, ties and precision at every output.** Preserve the stated equal-judge weighting, not weighting by the number of valid trials. Specify Task B’s row means, pooling and reported judge-level outputs explicitly. Distinguish (i) mean of trial ranks, (ii) position after sorting mean ranks, and (iii) frequency of being ranked first. A consensus winner need not be anyone’s frequent first choice. List all joint winners rather than breaking ties by file order. For enumeration use exact scaled integer/rational comparisons, count **all** scores equal to the observed score in the tail, include the observed assignment once, and compare unrounded fractions to thresholds. Supply the trial-to-option crosswalk, inclusion flags, exclusions and key with the rankings. A second implementation cannot resolve an unspecified estimand.
