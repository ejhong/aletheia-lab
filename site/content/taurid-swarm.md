## What we figured out {menu: Findings}

In our [Younger Dryas topic synthesis](../../topics/younger-dryas/), six locked paleoclimate studies ruled out a single catastrophic day that burned 9% of Earth's biomass 12,900 years ago. The most physically serious surviving impact scenario is the **Taurid breakup-comet hypothesis** (Clube & Napier 1984; Napier 2010): a giant 50–100 km comet entered the inner solar system 20,000–30,000 years ago and fragmented into Comet `2P/Encke`, the autumn and daytime Taurid meteor streams, and a denser **7:2 Jovian mean-motion resonant swarm** of large dark fragments that Earth crosses periodically.

Napier (2010) backed that model with a striking astronomical finding: among the brightest near-Earth asteroids (`H < 16.5`) sharing Comet Encke's rough orbital size, shape, and tilt, longitudes of perihelion (`ϖ`) clumped around Encke's `161°` (`p ~ 1/2000`). Today NASA's catalog holds **more than six times as many near-Earth asteroids (`43,156` NEAs)** as in 2010, and NASA's CNEOS archive holds **32 complete years (`1994–2025`) of global US satellite bolide detections (`1,045` airbursts, `1,182 kt` total energy)** spanning six published 7:2 swarm return years.

We locked four tests across both live NASA/JPL archives before inspecting any Taurid orbital or seasonal numbers.

Four things came out.

1. **Napier's (2010) alignment suffers a stark size inversion—the opposite of a broken comet.** At Napier's exact 2010 cutoff (`H < 16.5`, `N = 17` giant asteroids), `11 of 17` (`64.7%`) still point within `±45°` of Encke (`c̄ = +0.557`, `p = 0.0005`). Yet step just 1.5 magnitudes deeper to **1-kilometer asteroids (`H < 18.0`, `N = 64`)**, and the signal **collapses to zero** (`c̄ = −0.001`, `p = 0.504`)—only **`9 of the 47`** newly included 1-km asteroids (`19.1%`, *below* the `25%` random baseline!) align with Encke. At `H < 20.0` (`N = 331`), `H < 22.0` (`N = 848`), and `All H` (`N = 2,470`), the aligned share stays stuck at **`19.6%–21.7%`** (`c̄ = −0.068`). A real fragmenting comet follows a steep cascade (`N(>D) ∝ D^(-3.5)`), producing *far more* aligned 400 m and 1 km fragments than 3 km fragments. NASA's catalog shows the exact reverse.
2. **Jupiter's gravity explains why moderate-eccentricity controls looked different.** Our moderate-eccentricity control box (`0.40 ≤ e < 0.63`) shows a massive alignment (`c̄ = −0.33`, `u = −13.7`) pointing straight at **Jupiter's perihelion (`ϖ_J = 14.7°`)**—opposite Encke. High-eccentricity planet-crossers (`e ≥ 0.63`) break free of Jupiter's apsidal lock equally in both the Taurid plane (`i ≤ 14°`) and the inclined control (`14° < i ≤ 35°`, `p = 0.37–0.78` between them at every tier `H < 18` through `All H`).
3. **Only a handful of sub-km dark bodies occupy the 7:2 Jupiter resonance.** Across all 43,156 NEAs, only **3** `H < 18.0` asteroids lie in the `2.21–2.30 AU` 7:2 Taurid resonant band—and two of those three (`4197 Morpheus`, albedo `0.37`; `6063 Jason`, albedo `0.21`) are bright stony asteroids, not dark cometary nuclei. At `H < 20.0` (`~350–600 m`), `13 of 72` aligned NEAs (`18.1%`) sit in `2.21–2.30 AU` vs. `11.2%` anti-Taurid (`1.61×`, `p = 0.095`) and `15.1%` in the secular control (`p = 0.302`)—only **6** of which meet tight Encke-like bounds (including genuinely dark comet-like `2004 TG10` and `2010 LU108`). Only down at the ~150 m sensitivity tier (`H < 22.0`) does a modest `~15`-object excess emerge (`20.7%` vs. `11.7%` anti, `p = 0.0025`).
4. **32 years of US satellite airbursts show zero Taurid or swarm-year excess.** Across `1,045` bolides (`1994–2025`), Autumn Taurids (`Oct 15–Nov 30`) record `133` vs. `134.5` expected (`0.99×`, `p = 0.568`; `≥ 1 kt`: `12` vs. `14.8`, `p = 0.820`), and Daytime β-Taurids (`Jun 10–Jul 10`) record `81` vs. `88.7` (`0.91×`, `p = 0.818`). Comparing the **6 published 7:2 resonant swarm return years (`1998, 2005, 2015, 2018, 2022, 2025`)** against the 26 non-swarm years using drift-immune within-year Autumn shares gives **`12.70%` vs. `12.96%` (`p = 0.520`)**, and `0.33` vs. `0.38` large (`≥ 1 kt`) bolides/year (`p = 0.688`).

## How it was done {menu: Methods}

- **Two live official NASA/JPL archives:** NASA/JPL Small-Body Database Query API (`jpl_neos.json`, `43,374` NEOs → `43,156` NEAs with valid `H`; `jpl_encke.json` for `2P/Encke`), and NASA/JPL CNEOS Fireball API (`cneos_fireballs.json`, `1,074` bolides from `1988–2026`, `1,045` in the 32 complete continuous-coverage years `1994–2025`). - **Locked first, zero amendments:** `protocol.md` was hashed and pushed to GitHub (`protocol.lock`, SHA-256 `a526349cbc5c`, `2026-11-23T19:42:27Z`) before computing any Taurid alignment or fireball statistics. Total paid spend: `$0`.

## What this cannot tell you {menu: Limits}

- **Sub-100-meter fragments.** Telescopic NEO catalogs remain incomplete below ~100 m (`H > 22.5`), though targeted ZTF and CFHT searches during the 2022 swarm encounter independently bounded the swarm to `≲ 10²` Tunguska-scale bodies—an impact rate below once per 4 million years (Ye et al. 2025; Wiegert et al. 2025).
- **What may have disintegrated 15,000 years ago.** A giant comet that broke apart 20–30 kyr ago could have lost volatile km-scale fragments to rapid sublimation before today, leaving only `2P/Encke`, dust, centimeter–decimeter meteoroids, and a few dark sub-km remnants (`2004 TG10`, `2005 UY6`, `2010 LU108`). What our locked tests rule out is **Napier's (2010) surviving km-scale asteroid swarm** and **any elevated meter-scale satellite airburst flux today**.
- **Whether past crossings left periodic paleoclimate marks.** That is queued separately in Issue [#104](https://github.com/ejhong/aletheia-lab/issues/104) (`taurid-holocene-cycles`).
