Steady level vs. spike excess

Did the Younger Dryas ammonium rise in NEEM come from a raised steady level or from short smoke spikes?

Entirely from the steady level. Yearly median ammonium jumped 2.58× (11.30 → 29.15 µg/kg, rank 1 of 28), while the excess spike component (mean minus median) actually fell from 0.87 to 0.59 µg/kg — putting 102% of the total rise in the steady background.

102% from steady level

Exact NGRIP replication (>5× spikes)

Does NEEM confirm NGRIP's exploratory finding of fewer sharp (>5×) fire-spike years after onset?

Yes, strongly. Years with a peak exceeding 5× the 51-year running median dropped nearly fourfold from 31 in the 150 years before onset (median 29.5) to 8 after onset (exact two-sided binomial p = 0.0003). Extreme >10× spikes fell from 18 to 0.

Confirmed (8 vs. 31, p = 0.0003)

Anti-denominator control (additive >50 µg/kg)

Could the higher baseline have masked a real surge in absolute wildfire plumes?

No. Counting years where the peak rises >50 µg/kg above the running baseline gives 18 years after onset vs. 14 before (median 14.5, ordinary rank 11 of 28, p = 0.48). The single largest plume after onset (162 µg/kg) was half as big as before (337 µg/kg), and far below warm early-Holocene boreal fire centuries (28–36 plumes/150 yr).

No plume surge (rank 11/28)

Snowfall-flux control (>50 µg/kg-eq)

Does correcting for the ~2× drop in Younger Dryas snowfall change the plume count?

No. Scaling each year's plume excess by NEEM's local 5-cm GICC05 annual layer thickness yields 16 flux-plume years after onset vs. 13 before (median 12.5, p = 0.58)—less than half the early-Holocene rate (34–58/150 yr).

Earlier glacial stadials (Pillar 2)

How does the Younger Dryas compare with earlier resolved abrupt coolings in NEEM?

Only 3 stadial onsets in NEEM retain ≥10 samples/yr (GS-1, GS-2.1c at 22.9 ka, GS-4 at 28.6 ka); deeper ice thins below 10 mm/yr. All three show steady-level jumps (2.50×, 1.48×, 3.46×) with zero spike surges.
