#19

Penrose and Gödel

Partly holds

The modest conclusion is proved; the proof of the strong one fails

Gödel's theorem shows that human mathematical insight is not the work of any machine.

If your mind were a particular machine, you could not also know that machine to be reliable. That much is proved. It does not follow that the mind is no machine: a machine can be reliable without being able to know it.

#66

The unfolding argument

Partly holds

Valid for experiments of fixed length only

Any system with feedback loops has a loop-free twin that behaves the same, so a theory tying consciousness to loops is false or untestable.

The twin exists for an experiment of fixed length, and needs one more part for every further step. With no limit on time there is no twin. All of it rests on behaviour being the only evidence about consciousness.

#48

Anaesthetics and microtubules

Could not tell

The method cannot pick out any anaesthetic target

Anaesthetics switch consciousness off by settling into pockets in the microtubules of nerve cells.

A computer fit of 35 gases into eight proteins shows nothing special about the microtubule protein. But it shows nothing special about the accepted targets either, and an unrelated protein does as well as any. The scores follow the size of the molecule.

#115

The 613 THz tubulin shift

Not found

Generic level repulsion; breaks on more gases and fails rotational symmetry

After binding, anaesthetics shift a 613 THz collective dipole mode in tubulin downward in proportion to potency while look-alike non-anaesthetics shift it upward (Craddock et al. 2017).

Egg-white lysozyme and 81 randomly placed rings reproduce the 8-versus-2 sign split identically to tubulin: 96% of all shifts follow simple two-oscillator level repulsion around 613 THz. On 27 anaesthetics and 7 look-alikes the split breaks down, and the 2017 equations fail 3D rotational symmetry.
