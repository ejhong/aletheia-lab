Pillar 1 · Dung spores in lake mud

Did large herbivores start collapsing before the Younger Dryas (12,896 years ago)?

Yes, in most cores — but not everywhere at once. All 9 of 9 high-spore cores peaked before the cold (median 14,950 BP, p = 0.0039), 9 of 11 dropped past half their peak beforehand (median 14,669 BP, 1,773 years early, Wilcoxon p = 0.0137), and 7 of 10 had their main step-down before the cold (median 13,940 BP). Yet 3 of 9 cores (Colorado, Texas, and one Indiana lake) stayed above 2% spores well into the Younger Dryas.

Started ~1,000–1,800 yr early

Pillar 2 · Radiocarbon plateau test

Could an instant wipeout at 12,850 years ago explain the younger fossils through radiocarbon smearing?

No. Across 2,000 simulated instant-wipeout catalogues run through IntCal20 with the exact lab errors, plateau smearing leaks at most 0.73% of probability mass into 12,700–11,700 BP (99th percentile). The real AMS record holds 4.95% — 6.8× higher (0 of 2,000 runs reach it, p < 0.0005). All six subsets and Surovell taphonomy correction give the same p < 0.0005 result.

Ruled out (p < 0.0005)

Pillar 2 · Direct bone dates around 12,800 BP

Do direct fossil dates drop before the Younger Dryas, or right at it?

Here the two archives diverge in an instructive way. Direct AMS bone dates do not fall before 13,000 BP — they surge to 1.92× Bølling–Allerød density during 13,500–12,900 BP, then plunge roughly tenfold between 12,800 and 12,600 BP — before settling into a real 700-to-1,900-year survival tail. Ten gold-standard Score-12 AMS collagen dates (5 mammoths, 3 mastodons, 1 giant beaver, 1 short-faced bear) each have >99.3% probability of living after 12,750 BP.

Sharp drop, clear survivors

Pillar 2 · Genus-by-genus extinction

Did North America's giant mammals disappear together or one by one?

One by one, over more than a millennium. On modern Score-12 AMS dates alone (dropping older 1960s–70s conventional outliers), horses (OLE 13,061 BP, 95% CI 12,715–13,143) and dire wolves (youngest 13,674 BP) vanish before or near the onset of the cold, while mammoths (OLE 11,852 BP, CI 10,295–12,150), mastodons (OLE 11,867 BP, CI 11,079–12,412), and Harrington's mountain goat (OLE 10,333 BP, CI 7,117–12,329) all survive centuries into the Younger Dryas.

Staggered (>1,200 yr span)
