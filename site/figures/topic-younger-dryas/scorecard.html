#43

Smoke in Greenland's ice

Partly

Unusual, but late and slow

A burst of fire chemicals at the moment the cold begins.

A real, century-long rise in ammonium in three ice cores. It starts 30 to 40 years after the cold and builds slowly.

#60

Is that rise a one-off?

Partly

The strong end of a common pattern

Nothing like it after the ice age's other sudden coolings.

Ammonium rises after most of 24 other coolings. The Younger Dryas rise is 2nd and 3rd largest of 25, in two cores.

#69

A burn across the planet

Not found

No sign of a global burn

A jolt in the carbon of the whole atmosphere, and charcoal worldwide.

The shift in Antarctic air is small, late and matched at other cold spells. The best-dated charcoal records show no peak.

#75

The cooling itself

Not found

An ordinary cooling, slow to start

A cooling sharper, deeper or more worldwide than the natural ones.

It began more slowly than 16 of 18 other coolings, reached an ordinary depth, and Antarctica did not cool.

#76

Platinum in the ice

Unclear

Platinum a little late; eruptions unusually heavy

The platinum layer at or just before the start of the cold.

It lies about 20 to 30 years after the cold began, though before the steepest fall. Four eruptions, two very large, came just before the cold.

#120

Chemistry of the platinum

Unclear

Mostly not an impact layer's chemistry

Where all six platinum-group elements were measured, they stand in a meteorite's proportions, as in the layer left by the asteroid that ended the dinosaurs.

At two sites, 23 of 27 samples look like ordinary crust or rhenium-rich mud and 4 lean towards the impact pattern. The largest spikes are not at the boundary. A small cosmic share is not ruled out.

#100

What one airburst leaves

Not found

A spike where it shows, not a steady rise

The one known airburst, Tunguska in 1908, marks the ice in the way the Younger Dryas ice is marked.

Across Greenland 1908 is an ordinary year for ammonium. At some sites there is a sharp, sooty spike, at others nothing. The Younger Dryas century has a steady rise and few spikes.

#114

Fire spikes in a second core

Not found

Raised background; no surge of big plumes

A century of repeated fires fills a second Greenland core with many more big smoke plumes. (A version we built ourselves; the authors describe one day.)

The rise in ammonium is a raised background, 2.6 times the level before. Years with a big plume: 18 after the cold began, 14 before. No tripling of fires; a modest rise is not ruled out.

#103

A swarm in the Taurid stream

Not found

No swarm of large fragments today

The stream the Earth crosses each autumn still carries a swarm of large fragments of the broken comet.

No sign of one among today's asteroids or in 32 years of airbursts seen by satellite. Seventeen large asteroids do line up with the comet, as was known in 2010; smaller ones do not.

#121

Comet dust in ocean mud

Unclear

No long dust cloud; one check on the method failed

A comet breaking up over thousands of years surrounds the Earth with fine dust, and helium-3 in sea-floor mud rises. (This cannot see a single day.)

No rise across 20,000 to 10,000 years ago in nine cores: the usual amount or slightly less. Solid for thousands of years; one core's evidence for centuries. One of three checks on the method failed.

#116

The great animals

Partly

Not sudden; a steep fall at the onset

In the 2007 version the animals die at the strike. The authors now say a blow at the onset, with losses over centuries.

Dung spores were already falling in most lake cores. Dated bones fall steeply as the cold begins, and ten well-dated animals lived 200 to 1,900 years on.

#113

Are the layers the same age?

Unclear

Dates disagree; most sites too loosely dated

Radiocarbon dates from the claimed layers agree on one age.

The precise dates do not agree, though charcoal older than its layer could explain that. Only 4 of 32 sites have a tight date on the layer itself.

#127

Two retracted papers, checked

Unclear

One paper's dates fail; the quartz grains are untested

Two papers from 2025 give new evidence of the layer: comet dust in Baffin Bay mud, and shocked quartz at three sites.

Dated as the paper itself describes, the Baffin Bay layers are 300 to 610 years too young. The quartz paper cannot show its grains belong to one layer of one age. Nobody has faulted the grains themselves.

#40

A crash in human numbers

Not found

No sign, though the test was blurred

Dated traces of people thin out at once, above all in North America.

No drop on three continents. But radiocarbon dating is at its most distorted in exactly those centuries.
