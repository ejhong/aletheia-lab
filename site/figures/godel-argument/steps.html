1. If I am the machine F, then everything F proves is true.uses: what is known is true
  2. Then F, with the statement "I am F" added to it, also proves only truths.uses rule one: whatever is so is true
  3. So the Gödel sentence of that enlarged system is true. Call it G.
  4. And the enlarged system cannot prove G.
  5. I have just shown "if I am F, then G". So I know it.uses rule two: whatever is proved is known
  6. But F cannot prove "if I am F, then G", by step 4.
  7. So if I am F, I know something that F cannot prove, and I am not F.
  8. Therefore I am not F.

Valid

From its two rules and six facts of arithmetic, step 8 follows. The logic has no gap.

But the rules clash

Each rule yields a contradiction once sentences can speak of their own truth. From such rules anything follows.

Repaired, it stops short

With safe rules it reaches only this: if I am F in my arithmetic, I know something F cannot prove.
