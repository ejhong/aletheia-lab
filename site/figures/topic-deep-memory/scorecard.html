The Australasian signal in the Amazon

Not found

Real, but not from across the Pacific

The resemblance between some Amazonians and Australasians traces to a crossing of the Pacific.

It is real and faint, and it leads back to mainland Asia tens of thousands of years ago. None of 58 groups of ancient Americans stands out for it.

#41

Strangers in the record

Not found

No strangers found

Somebody before Columbus lies buried on the wrong side of an ocean.

Among 12,977 ancient people nobody does, apart from the medieval Norse of Greenland. The one person left unexplained was checked in her raw DNA, which points to modern contamination.

#85

The hole in the ancient sky

Not found

The hole dates the catalogue, not the constellations

The empty southern patch in the classical constellations is centred where the pole stood long before Babylon or Greece.

It is centred where the pole stood for Hipparchus and Ptolemy, who compiled the catalogue. A sky of 3000 BCE fits far worse. But a catalogue's edge dates its last compilers, not the first people to draw the figures.

#92

The star map on a pillar

Could not tell

Blind machine judges were not up to it

The animals on Pillar 43 at Göbekli Tepe match constellations so well that chance is ruled out.

Three vision models, shown the paper's pictures blind, could not match even the scorpion to its constellation, so they could not serve as judges. The paper's odds rest on its authors' own ranking, which nobody else has checked.

#44

Flood myths and drowned land

Not found

No sign of it, in a test weaker than it first looked

Flood stories are commonest among peoples whose coast lost the most land when the Ice Age sea rose.

Among 396 peoples near water in a catalogue of world myth, flood motifs are as common beside the narrowest drowned shelves (36%) as beside the widest (34%). The test had faults, one of its two checks on the method failed, and it could have missed a modest effect; mending the faults left the answer the same.

#82

Smoke from lost furnaces

Could not tell

Too coarse to see early metalworking

A civilisation that smelted metal before the Bronze Age left lead in the ice and peat of its time.

Nothing unusual before 5,000 years ago. But the same records show none of the smelting known from 7,000 years ago, and the test as fixed would have missed Rome. No northern data for most of the Ice Age.

#88

Crop pollen in Ice Age mud

Could not tell

The detector failed its own test

Farming before 12,000 years ago left cereal pollen, field weeds and cleared forest in lake cores.

No such signal. But the detector found 23% of known farming-era cores where the plan required half, and none in the Fertile Crescent when farming began there.

#15

The Secret Gospel of Mark

Partly

An unusually close echo of Mark; author unknown

The letter that quotes it is by Clement of Alexandria, and the quoted passages are an early part of Mark.

The letter is too short for style to say who wrote it. The quoted passages share more phrases with Mark, word for word, than any stretch of Mark shares with the rest: what a close imitation would look like, with no date on it.

#12

Linear A read as Semitic

Could not tell

The test cannot decide

Read with the proposed sound values, the words of Minoan Crete are Semitic.

They match Semitic roots as often as Greek words do, and so do Hebrew words. The test cannot tell a Semitic language from Greek, and the proposal's main evidence was not tested.
