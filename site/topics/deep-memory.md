## The idea

Bird-headed men, feathered serpents, teachers who arrive by sea and bring the arts of civilisation, a flood that ends a world, a stone that marks the navel of the earth. The same forms turn up in ancient cultures that, on the usual account, never met.

The atlas's [Deep Memory](https://ejhong.github.io/aletheia/cases/deep-memory/) case asks how much of that resemblance belongs to a shared past, and how much to the ways people everywhere make images, tell stories and look for connections. That is a question about texts and images, and the atlas weighs it.

The lab comes at it from the other side. A shared past is a physical claim. Either people travelled between the cultures, or knowledge was handed down across a very long stretch of time. Travellers leave descendants, and descendants carry their ancestry in their bones. Old astronomy leaves a date in the sky, because the stars drift in a way that can be calculated. Neither kind of trace depends on reading a myth the right way.

## Did anyone cross?

Stories of lost seafarers and of contact between the Old World and the New before Columbus all make the same prediction. People who travel and settle leave descendants. Thousands of ancient people have now had their DNA read and published. If the travellers existed, some of them should be there.

- **The Australasian signal in the Amazon.** A few Amazonian peoples do carry a faint resemblance to New Guineans and Aboriginal Australians, and it is real. But it leads back to mainland Asia tens of thousands of years ago, and came into the Americas over land with the first settlers. It does not point to a voyage across the Pacific.
- **Strangers in the record.** Among nearly thirteen thousand ancient people, nobody before Columbus is buried on the wrong side of an ocean, apart from the medieval Norse of Greenland, whom history already knew. The one person the search could not explain turned out, on a check of her raw DNA, to carry modern contamination.

{{figure: strangers/map}}

Both searches found what was already known, which shows they could see. Neither found anything else.

## Is the map of the sky older than history?

The Earth's axis wobbles once in about 26,000 years, and the pole of the sky moves among the stars as it does. So anything mapped from the stars, or aligned to them, carries a clock. Writers on lost civilisations have often claimed that these clocks read 10,500 BCE or thereabouts.

{{figure: topic-deep-memory/sky-plate}}

One clock has been read so far. The classical constellations leave a patch of the southern sky empty, centred on where the south pole stood for whoever set its edge. The date it gives is that of the Greek astronomers who compiled the star catalogue, between about 130 BCE and 140 CE.

That is the right answer to the question the record can answer, and the wrong question for the purpose. A cataloguer lists every star he can see, so the edge of the listed stars is his own horizon. The figures those stars belong to may be far older, and the catalogue is silent on it. The study also showed how soft such dates are: the model named in advance as the main one put the catalogue in 910 CE, centuries after it was written, because of one assumption about haze near the horizon.

Read more: [the hole in the ancient sky](../../questions/ancient-sky-hole/), where the sky can be wound back by hand.

## What the studies add up to

Three tests, two kinds of evidence, the same result: where a shared past should have left a physical trace that we could look for, the trace is not there. The DNA shows no travellers. The one star clock read so far shows the date of the people known to have set it.

That is not the same as showing the resemblances are coincidence. All three tests have blind spots, set out below, and none of them touches the stories and images themselves.

Waiting their turn: whether the bringers of civilisation in old stories came from the sea, whether flood stories cluster where land was drowned, whether basic words are shared across the oceans, whether germs and parasites tell a different story from the bones, and two more star clocks, a carved pillar at Göbekli Tepe and the orientations of ancient monuments.

## What this does not settle

- **The resemblances themselves.** Whether a bird-man in one place is the same idea as a bird-man in another is the atlas's question, not the lab's.
- **Travellers who left no sampled descendants.** Ancient DNA sees only the people who happen to have been dug up and sequenced. Egypt, Mesopotamia and the Indus valley are almost empty in the public record.
- **How old the constellations are.** A test of the figures' own age would need what the oldest sources say about them as wholes, not the positions of single catalogued stars.
- **Knowledge passed on without people or star maps:** by word of mouth, which leaves no physical trace at all.
- **The limits of the work itself.** AI systems designed, ran and replicated every study. No specialist has reviewed any of it.
