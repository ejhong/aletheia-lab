## The idea

About 12,850 years ago the warming at the end of the last ice age went into reverse. Within decades the north was cold again, and stayed so for more than a thousand years. That cold spell is the Younger Dryas.

The impact hypothesis says a comet caused it: fragments struck or burst over North America, lit fires across continents, darkened the sky, and brought down the Clovis people and the great Ice Age animals with the climate. It is one of the main supports for the larger idea of a lost world destroyed in a cataclysm.

An event like that should leave marks in several records that have nothing to do with one another. Each study here takes one of those marks, fixes the test before looking, and asks whether the mark is there.

## What the studies add up to

- **Smoke in Greenland's ice.** There is a real mark: about a century of unusually high ammonium, in three ice cores. But it begins thirty to forty years after the cold set in, and it builds slowly. Fire from the sky should mark the ice at once.
- **The same test at other sudden coolings.** The ice age had two dozen of them. After most, ammonium rises too. The Younger Dryas rise is among the largest, and two much older coolings come close to it. It is the strong end of something the ice does again and again.
- **A burn across the whole planet.** Antarctic air, which mixes the whole atmosphere, and the best-dated charcoal records worldwide show no sign of one. The shift in the air's carbon is small, late, and matched at other cold spells.
- **A crash in human numbers.** Being tested now.

So far the pattern is consistent. The records do hold real disturbances around the start of the Younger Dryas. Each one looks like what a sudden change of climate does by itself, here and at other times, and none is the sharp, one-off, simultaneous mark that the hypothesis predicts.

## What this does not settle

None of these studies looks for the impact itself. The physical traces claimed for it, such as platinum, tiny spherules and nanodiamonds in a thin layer of sediment, are not tested here, and neither is the fate of the large animals. What has been tested is the consequences claimed for the impact: the fires, and soon the loss of people. A comet that struck without setting the continents alight is not ruled out by anything on this page.
