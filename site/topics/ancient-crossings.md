## The idea

Stories of lost seafarers, of civilisers who arrived by ship, and of contact between the Old World and the New before Columbus all make a prediction that does not depend on ruins or legends. People who travel and settle leave descendants, and descendants carry their ancestry in their bones. Thousands of ancient people have now had their DNA read and published. If the travellers existed, some of them should be there.

## What the studies add up to

- **The Australasian signal in the Amazon.** A few Amazonian peoples do carry a faint resemblance to New Guineans and Aboriginal Australians, and it is real. But it leads back to mainland Asia tens of thousands of years ago, and came into the Americas over land with the first settlers. It does not point to a voyage across the Pacific.
- **Strangers in the record.** Among nearly thirteen thousand ancient people, nobody before Columbus is buried on the wrong side of an ocean, apart from the medieval Norse of Greenland, whom history already knew. The one person the search could not explain turned out, on a check of her raw DNA, to carry modern contamination.

Both searches found what was already known, which shows they could see. Neither found anything else.

Two further questions are waiting: whether the bringers of civilisation in old stories came from the sea, and whether germs and parasites, which travel with people, tell a different story from the bones.

## What this does not settle

Ancient DNA sees only the people who happen to have been dug up and sequenced. Egypt, Mesopotamia and the Indus valley are almost empty in the public record. Visitors who left no descendants among the sampled dead are invisible. Where the record can see, it sees no strangers; where it cannot, nothing has been shown either way.
